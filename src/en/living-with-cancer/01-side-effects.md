## First: when to get help straight away

During treatment, get help at once (following your team's instructions, or at an emergency department) if you have:

- A **temperature** of 38 °C or more, or shivering, especially during chemotherapy.
- **Breathlessness**, chest pain or a swollen leg.
- Severe or persistent **diarrhoea**, especially with immunotherapy.
- Vomiting that stops you drinking, bleeding, confusion or new weakness.

## Common effects and what helps

| Effect | What can help |
|---|---|
| **Tiredness** (fatigue) | The most common. Surprisingly, moderate physical activity improves it. Pace your day, rest without spending all day in bed, and ask about anaemia or thyroid problems |
| **Nausea and vomiting** | Now very well prevented with anti-sickness drugs before treatment. Small, frequent meals, cold foods without strong smells |
| **Mouth sores** (mucositis) | Gentle brushing, alcohol-free mouthwashes, avoiding acidic or spicy foods |
| **Diarrhoea or constipation** | Fluids, diet changes and medication as needed. Report it if severe |
| **Hair loss** | With some drugs. It usually grows back. Scalp cooling reduces it with some regimens |
| **Low immunity** | Hand washing, avoiding people with infections, following any food safety advice you are given |
| **Tingling** in hands and feet (neuropathy) | Report it early, because the dose may need adjusting |
| **Skin** | Moisturising, sun protection. Some targeted therapies cause rashes that can be treated |
| **Taste and appetite changes** | Try new flavours, eat when hungry, see a dietitian if you lose weight |
| **Brain fog** | Difficulty concentrating or remembering. It usually improves with time; lists and physical activity help |

## Effects by treatment

- **Chemotherapy**: those in the table, depending on the drug.
- **Radiotherapy**: mainly in the treated area (skin, mucous membranes, bowel, bladder) and tiredness.
- **Immunotherapy**: inflammation of different organs, which may appear months later. Report any new symptom.
- **Hormone therapy**: hot flushes, joint pain, bone loss, sexual changes.
- **Surgery**: pain, lymphoedema after lymph node removal, changes in body image.

## Talk about everything

Effects on **sex life**, mood or body image are sometimes left unsaid out of embarrassment, but they can be treated or eased. Take a list of symptoms to each appointment and ask. Your oncology nurses are great allies.
