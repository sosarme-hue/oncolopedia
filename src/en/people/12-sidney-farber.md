## A pathologist among children

Sidney Farber was a pathologist at Boston Children's Hospital. In the 1940s, **acute childhood leukaemia** was a death sentence: children died within weeks, with no possible treatment.

## Aminopterin

Folic acid was known to be needed to make blood cells. Farber first tried giving folate to children with leukaemia, and the disease got worse. So he thought of the opposite: blocking folate. With **antifolate** drugs synthesised by Yellapragada SubbaRow and the Lederle company, such as **aminopterin**, he treated 16 children. In 1948 he reported that 10 had **temporary remissions**.

The remissions lasted months and the children eventually relapsed, and many colleagues received the work with scepticism. But it was the first proof that a drug could make a cancer retreat. Its successor, **methotrexate**, is still used today.

## More than a scientist

Farber championed whole-person care for children and their families, and was a tireless fundraiser and advocate for cancer research, helping pass the US National Cancer Act of 1971. The cancer institute he founded in Boston is now called **Dana-Farber**.

Today about 90% of children with acute lymphoblastic leukaemia are cured with chemotherapy combinations that are heirs to his work.
