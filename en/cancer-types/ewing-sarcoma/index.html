Cancer types

Ewing sarcoma

A bone and soft tissue tumour of children and young people, defined by the EWSR1-FLI1 fusion. It requires intensive chemotherapy, surgery and radiotherapy.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common Second most common bone tumour in young people; median age 15
Where Pelvis, femur, ribs, spine, also soft tissue
Alteration EWSR1-FLI1 fusion (85%) or other EWSR1 fusions
Symptoms Pain, swelling, sometimes fever (mistaken for infection)
Treatment Chemotherapy (VDC/IE), surgery and/or radiotherapy
Survival 70-75% localised; 30% with metastases

Treatment

Intensive chemotherapy in alternating cycles (vincristine, doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide with ifosfamide and etoposide, interval-compressed; or VIDE), local control with surgery and/or radiotherapy (it is a radiosensitive tumour), and further chemotherapy. At relapse: irinotecan with temozolomide, high-dose chemotherapy, clinical trials.

Round cell sarcomas with other fusions (CIC, BCOR) are now considered separate entities.

See also: Osteosarcoma, Sarcomas, Childhood cancer.
