Cancer types

Head and neck cancer

Cancers of the mouth, throat, larynx and nearby areas, linked to tobacco, alcohol and, increasingly, the human papillomavirus.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
Sites Mouth, oropharynx (tonsils, base of tongue), larynx, hypopharynx, nasopharynx, nasal cavity and salivary glands
Most common type Squamous cell carcinoma
Causes Tobacco and alcohol (together they multiply the risk), HPV in the oropharynx, Epstein-Barr virus in the nasopharynx
Signs A mouth ulcer that has not healed in three weeks, persistent hoarseness, difficulty swallowing, a lump in the neck

What it is

This term covers cancers arising in the linings of the head and neck. Almost all are squamous cell carcinomas. They are treated by teams including head and neck surgeons, oncologists, radiation oncologists, speech therapists, dietitians and dentists, because they affect speaking, swallowing and breathing.

Causes

  • Tobacco and alcohol: the classic cause. Together they multiply the risk.
  • Human papillomavirus (HPV): causes a growing share of oropharyngeal cancers (tonsils and base of tongue), often in younger non-smokers. These have a better outlook, and HPV vaccination should reduce them in future.
  • Epstein-Barr virus: linked to nasopharyngeal cancer, common in southern China and South-East Asia.
  • Chewing betel quid, common in South Asia, and some workplace exposures.

Symptoms

See a doctor if any of these lasts more than three weeks:

  • An ulcer, white or red patch in the mouth that does not heal.
  • Hoarseness or voice change.
  • Pain or difficulty swallowing, one-sided earache.
  • A lump in the neck that does not go away.
  • One-sided nasal blockage or bleeding.

Diagnosis and treatment

Endoscopic examination, biopsy, CT or MRI and PET-CT. In the oropharynx, the tumour is tested for HPV (p16 marker), which changes stage and outlook.

Depending on site and stage, surgery (including transoral robotic surgery), radiotherapy and chemotherapy are used, often combined. In recurrent or metastatic disease, anti-PD-1 immunotherapy. Voice and swallowing rehabilitation, mouth care and nutrition are essential.

See also: Tobacco, alcohol and other risks, Vaccines that prevent cancer.
