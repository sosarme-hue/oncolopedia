## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 1-3% of head and neck cancers |
| **Where** | Parotid (70% of tumours, 20-25% malignant), submandibular, minor glands (more often malignant) |
| **Types** | Over 20: mucoepidermoid, adenoid cystic, acinic cell, salivary duct carcinoma... |
| **Sign** | A lump in front of the ear or under the jaw; facial palsy (a sign of malignancy) |
| **Treatment** | Surgery preserving the facial nerve; radiotherapy; targeted therapy by type |

## Main types

- **Mucoepidermoid carcinoma**: the most common; low to high grade.
- **Adenoid cystic carcinoma**: slow-growing but invades nerves and recurs late, sometimes in the lung after many years.
- **Acinic cell carcinoma** and secretory carcinoma (with an *ETV6-NTRK3* fusion).
- **Salivary duct carcinoma**: aggressive; often with **androgen receptors** and **HER2**.
- The most common benign tumour is **pleomorphic adenoma**, best removed because it can become malignant over years.

## Diagnosis and treatment

- Ultrasound with **needle sampling**, MRI.
- **Parotidectomy** with monitoring and preservation of the **facial nerve** whenever it is not invaded.
- **Radiotherapy** after surgery for high-grade tumours or involved margins.
- Advanced disease: **anti-androgen** and **anti-HER2** therapy (trastuzumab, trastuzumab deruxtecan) for salivary duct carcinoma; **larotrectinib or entrectinib** for *NTRK* fusions; lenvatinib for adenoid cystic.
