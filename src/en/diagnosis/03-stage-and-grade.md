## Two different ideas

- **Stage** describes how far the cancer has **spread** in the body: the size of the tumour, whether it affects lymph nodes and whether it has reached other organs.
- **Grade** describes how the **cells look** under the microscope: the more different they are from normal cells, the higher the grade and, generally, the faster they grow.

A tumour can be small and localised (low stage) but high grade, or the other way round. Both help decide treatment.

## The TNM system

The most widely used staging system for solid tumours, produced by the AJCC and the UICC:

- **T** (tumour): size and local extent, from T1 to T4. *Tis* is carcinoma in situ.
- **N** (nodes): from N0 (no nodes affected) to N3.
- **M** (metastasis): M0 (no distant spread) or M1.

The combination is grouped into **stages 0 to IV**:

| Stage | General meaning |
|---|---|
| **0** | Carcinoma in situ: has not invaded |
| **I** | Small, localised tumour |
| **II-III** | Larger tumour or regional lymph nodes affected |
| **IV** | Metastases in other organs |

A "c" in front (cTNM) means **clinical** stage, from examination and imaging; a "p" (pTNM) means **pathological** stage, after examining the surgical specimen; a "y" (ypTNM), after earlier treatment.

## Not all cancers use TNM

- **Leukaemias** are not staged this way, because they are in the blood from the start; they are classified by their genetic changes and risk.
- **Lymphomas** use the Lugano classification (stages I to IV by the lymph node regions involved).
- **Myeloma** uses the R-ISS system.
- **Brain tumours** are not staged: type, grade and molecular profile are what matter.
- Gynaecological tumours also use the **FIGO** system.

## Grade

Often given from 1 to 3 (well, moderately or poorly differentiated). Some tumours have their own systems: **Gleason** and ISUP grade groups in prostate, Nottingham in breast, the WHO grades 1 to 4 in brain tumours.

## What it does not mean

An advanced stage does not mean there is no treatment. Many stage IV cancers are controlled for years, and some, such as testicular cancer or certain lymphomas, are often cured.
