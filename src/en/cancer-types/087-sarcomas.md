## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | About 1% of adult cancers and 10-15% of childhood cancers; about 5,300 a year in the UK |
| **Types** | Soft tissue (80%) and bone (20%); over 70 subtypes |
| **Sign** | A deep **lump**, over 5 cm or growing, even if painless |
| **Golden rule** | Biopsy and treatment in a **reference centre** before surgery |
| **Treatment** | Surgery with wide margins, radiotherapy, chemotherapy depending on type |
| **Survival** | About 60-65% at five years for soft tissue |

## What they are

**Sarcomas** arise from cells of the **mesenchyme**, the embryonic tissue that forms muscle, fat, bone, cartilage, vessels and fibrous tissue. They can appear anywhere in the body, mainly in the **limbs** (thigh), retroperitoneum, trunk and head and neck.

## Risk factors

Most have no known cause. Factors: **previous radiotherapy** (radiation-induced sarcomas years later), inherited syndromes (**Li-Fraumeni**, neurofibromatosis type 1, hereditary retinoblastoma), chronic lymphoedema (angiosarcoma), vinyl chloride.

## Symptoms

- A growing **lump**, often painless, mistaken for a bruise or lipoma.
- Persistent **bone pain**, especially at night, or a fracture (bone sarcomas).
- Vague abdominal symptoms in retroperitoneal tumours, which can grow very large.

**Referral criteria**: any soft tissue lump **over 5 cm**, **deep** to the fascia, **growing** or recurring after removal should be investigated with MRI and biopsy in a specialist centre.

## Diagnosis

1. **MRI** (or CT for the abdomen).
2. **Core needle biopsy** planned by the team who will operate (a poorly done biopsy can compromise surgery).
3. Review by an **expert sarcoma pathologist** with molecular testing: up to 25% of diagnoses change.
4. Chest CT (sarcomas mainly spread to the lungs).

## Treatment

- **Surgery** with wide margins, saving the limb in over 90% of cases.
- **Radiotherapy**, preferably before surgery for limb sarcomas.
- **Chemotherapy** depending on type: essential for Ewing, osteosarcoma and rhabdomyosarcoma; for adults with high-risk soft tissue sarcoma decided case by case (doxorubicin and ifosfamide).
- Advanced disease: doxorubicin, trabectedin, eribulin (liposarcoma), pazopanib, gemcitabine with docetaxel; targeted therapy for specific subtypes (larotrectinib for *NTRK* fusions, afami-cel, a T-cell therapy, for synovial sarcoma).
- **Surgery for lung metastases** in selected patients.

## Why a reference centre

Studies show that patients treated in high-volume centres with **multidisciplinary teams** have fewer relapses and better survival. In the UK, sarcoma care is organised through specialist sarcoma centres, and in Europe through the **EURACAN** network.

## Questions for your team

- What exact subtype of sarcoma do I have? Has an expert pathologist reviewed it?
- Will radiotherapy be before or after surgery?
- Do I need chemotherapy?
- What will rehabilitation involve?
