## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Children under 10 and teenagers |
| **Where** | Head and neck (orbit), genitourinary tract, limbs |
| **Types** | Embryonal (better outlook) and alveolar (*PAX3/7-FOXO1* fusion, worse) |
| **Treatment** | Chemotherapy (VAC or IVA), surgery and/or radiotherapy |
| **Survival** | 70% overall; over 90% for low risk |

## Key points

- **Embryonal** occurs in young children; the botryoid subtype grows like a bunch of grapes in the vagina or bladder.
- **Alveolar** is more common in teenagers, in the limbs, and has a worse outlook.
- Linked to syndromes such as Li-Fraumeni, neurofibromatosis type 1 and DICER1.
- Treatment with **chemotherapy** (vincristine, actinomycin D, cyclophosphamide or ifosfamide), **local control** with surgery and/or radiotherapy (protons in young children), and maintenance with vinorelbine and cyclophosphamide for high risk.
