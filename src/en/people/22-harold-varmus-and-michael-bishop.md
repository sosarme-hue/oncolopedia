## A gene from the Rous virus

Rous sarcoma virus, discovered in 1911, caused cancer in chickens through a gene called ***src***. At the University of California, San Francisco, the virologist **J. Michael Bishop** and the young physician **Harold Varmus** wanted to know where that gene came from.

## The surprise

In 1976 they reported that a version of *src* was present in the DNA of **normal** chicken **cells**, and later in many other species, including humans. The virus had not brought a foreign gene: it had **captured** one from a cell at some point and altered it. It was proof that cancer genes lie within us, as **proto-oncogenes** that regulate normal growth, and that mutation turns them into **oncogenes**.

## A new way to see cancer

The finding opened the search for human oncogenes. In 1982 the first, the ***RAS*** gene, was identified in a bladder cancer, partly thanks to the work of Mariano Barbacid. Today many treatments act against proteins made by oncogenes. Varmus and Bishop received the Nobel Prize in 1989. Varmus later directed the US National Institutes of Health and National Cancer Institute.
