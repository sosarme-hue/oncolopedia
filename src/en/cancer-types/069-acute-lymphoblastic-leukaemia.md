## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 25% of childhood cancers; peak at 2 to 5 years; second peak over 60 |
| **Types** | B (85%) and T (15%); Philadelphia-positive (25% of adults) |
| **Treatment** | 2-3 years of chemotherapy; tyrosine kinase inhibitors if Ph+; blinatumomab, inotuzumab, CAR-T, transplant |
| **Cure** | About 90% in children; 40-60% in adults, improving |

## What it is

A build-up of **lymphoblasts** (immature lymphoid cells) in the marrow. Genetic subtypes have very different outlooks: hyperdiploidy and *ETV6-RUNX1* (favourable, common in children), **Philadelphia** chromosome (*BCR-ABL1*) and Philadelphia-like, *KMT2A* rearrangements in infants.

## Symptoms

Tiredness, pallor, fever, infections, bruising, bone pain (children may limp or refuse to walk), swollen glands, enlarged spleen. T-ALL may present with a chest mass pressing on the airway.

## Treatment

- **Chemotherapy** in phases (induction, consolidation, intensification, maintenance) over 2-3 years, with chemotherapy into the spinal fluid to protect the nervous system.
- **Minimal residual disease (MRD)**: measuring remaining leukaemia cells with very sensitive techniques guides treatment intensity.
- **Philadelphia-positive**: **imatinib, dasatinib or ponatinib**; ponatinib with blinatumomab combinations with little or no chemotherapy.
- **Blinatumomab** (CD19-CD3 bispecific antibody) added to first-line treatment in children and adults improves survival (E1910 and AALL1731 trials).
- **Inotuzumab ozogamicin**; **CAR-T** (tisagenlecleucel, brexucabtagene) at relapse.
- **Allogeneic transplant** for high risk or relapse.
