## What to expect

The first appointment is usually long and full of information. The oncologist will review your history and test results, examine you and explain what is known about the tumour, what is still unknown and what the options are. Often the final plan is not decided that day: more tests are awaited or the case goes to the **multidisciplinary team** (tumour board), a meeting of surgeons, oncologists, radiation oncologists, pathologists and radiologists who decide together.

It is normal to leave remembering only half of what was said. That is why preparing helps.

## Before you go

- **Documents**: reports from the emergency department, other specialists, pathology and imaging (bring scans on a disc or app if you have them).
- A **list of your medicines**, including herbal products and supplements, and your allergies.
- **Other illnesses** and previous operations.
- **Family history** of cancer: who, which type and at what age.
- **Your questions written down**, in order of importance.
- A **notebook** or your phone to take notes. You can ask permission to record the explanation.

## Take someone with you

If you can, go with someone you trust. They help you listen, remember and ask what you might not dare to. Agree beforehand what role you want them to play.

## Questions that often help

**About the diagnosis**
- What type of cancer do I have and what stage is it?
- Are more tests needed? When will we have all the results?

**About treatment**
- What is the aim: to cure, to control the disease or to relieve symptoms?
- What are the options and why do you recommend this one?
- How long does it last, how often will I come in and which side effects are most likely?
- Is there a clinical trial for my situation?
- Will it affect my fertility, my work or my sex life?

**About practicalities**
- Who do I call if I have a problem? Is there a nurse helpline?
- When is my next appointment?
- Where can I find reliable information?

## After the appointment

Go over your notes the same day. If something was unclear, write it down for next time or call the oncology nurse. If you face an important decision, you are entitled to take time to think: see [Shared decisions and second opinions](../shared-decisions-and-second-opinions/).
