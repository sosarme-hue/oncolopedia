## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cause** | **Human herpesvirus 8** (HHV-8 or KSHV) with a weakened immune system |
| **Forms** | Classic (older Mediterranean men), HIV-associated, African endemic, in transplant recipients |
| **Appearance** | Purple patches or nodules on skin, mouth, legs; may affect organs |
| **Treatment** | Antiretroviral treatment, reducing immunosuppression; chemotherapy (liposomal doxorubicin, paclitaxel), pomalidomide |

## What it is

It was described by Hungarian dermatologist **Moritz Kaposi** in 1872 in older men. In 1981, its appearance in young men in New York and California was one of the first signs of **AIDS**. In 1994 Yuan Chang and Patrick Moore discovered the virus that causes it, HHV-8.

## Forms

- **Classic**: in older men of Mediterranean, Jewish or Eastern European origin; very slow-growing leg lesions.
- **HIV-associated (epidemic)**: more extensive and aggressive; much reduced thanks to antiretrovirals.
- **Endemic**: in sub-Saharan Africa, including children.
- **Iatrogenic**: in transplant recipients or on immunosuppressive treatment.

## Treatment

- **HIV**: **antiretroviral treatment** alone makes many lesions disappear.
- **Transplant recipients**: reducing immunosuppression or switching to sirolimus.
- Localised lesions: radiotherapy, cryotherapy, laser, topical alitretinoin, intralesional vinblastine or surgery.
- Extensive disease: **liposomal doxorubicin**, **paclitaxel**, **pomalidomide**.

## Outlook

The classic form rarely shortens life. In people with well-treated HIV survival is good; extensive lung or gut involvement is more serious.
