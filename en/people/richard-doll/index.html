Richard Doll

The British epidemiologist who, with Austin Bradford Hill, showed that smoking causes lung cancer. His British doctors study lasted 50 years.

A mysterious epidemic

After the Second World War, deaths from lung cancer had soared in the United Kingdom. Road tar, pollution and the 1918 flu were suspected. In 1948 the Medical Research Council commissioned a study from the statistician Austin Bradford Hill and the young doctor Richard Doll.

Cases and controls

They interviewed patients with lung cancer and patients admitted for other reasons. Almost all the cancer patients smoked, and the risk rose with the number of cigarettes. In 1950 they reported in the British Medical Journal that smoking was "a factor, and an important factor" in lung cancer. Doll, a smoker, gave up during the study.

The British doctors study

To confirm the finding, in 1951 they wrote to all doctors in the United Kingdom about their smoking habits and followed more than 34,000 for 50 years. They showed that smoking causes lung cancer and many other diseases, that smokers lose on average about 10 years of life, and that stopping smoking at any age gives back much of it.

A methodological legacy

In 1965 Bradford Hill set out the criteria for causation still used to judge whether an association is causal. Doll also studied the effects of radiation, asbestos and alcohol.

See also: Tobacco, alcohol and other risks, Lung cancer.
