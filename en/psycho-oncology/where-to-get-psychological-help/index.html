Psycho-oncology

Where to get psychological help

Resources for people with cancer and their families: the hospital's psycho-oncology unit, primary care, charities offering free support and crisis lines, in Spain, the UK and the US.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

Where to start

  1. Your oncology team: tell your oncologist or nurse. Many hospitals have a psycho-oncology unit or can refer you to clinical psychology or psychiatry.
  2. Your GP: can assess how you are, start treatment if needed and refer you to mental health services.
  3. Palliative care: if the illness is advanced, the team includes psychological support for the patient and family.
  4. Social workers at the hospital or health centre: advise on resources and support.

Free support from charities

Country Resource
Spain Spanish Association Against Cancer (AECC): free psychological care for patients and relatives, in person, by phone and online. Infocáncer: 900 100 036, 24 hours
United Kingdom Macmillan Cancer Support: 0808 808 00 00; Maggie's centres; Cancer Research UK nurses
United States American Cancer Society: 1-800-227-2345; CancerCare offers free counselling
Everywhere Patient associations for each type of cancer: support groups and contact with people who have been through the same

If it is urgent

  • Spain: 024, the Ministry of Health's suicide prevention line, free, confidential and available 24 hours.
  • UK and Ireland: Samaritans, 116 123.
  • United States: 988, Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text).
  • Emergencies: 112 in Europe, 999 in the UK, 911 in the US, if there is immediate danger.
  • The emergency department of your hospital.

Choosing a private professional

  • Check that they are a registered clinical or health psychologist (in Spain, a psicólogo sanitario or specialist in clinical psychology).
  • Ask about their experience with cancer patients or with grief.
  • Ask about the approach they use: therapies with evidence include cognitive behavioural therapy and mindfulness-based or meaning-centred interventions.
  • Agree on the number of sessions and the goals from the start.

More information

Psycholopedia is our sister encyclopedia of psychology, and the Spanish Psycho-oncology Society (SEPO) brings together professionals in the field. See also What is psycho-oncology and Emotional support.
