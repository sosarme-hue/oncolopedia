Cancer types

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

The most common lymphoma. It is aggressive, but overall about two in three patients are cured with immunochemotherapy, with differences by stage and age. CAR-T and bispecific antibodies are options at relapse.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common 30-40% of non-Hodgkin lymphomas; median age 65
Presentation Nodes or masses growing over weeks; B symptoms; sometimes outside the nodes
Outlook IPI score; cell-of-origin subtypes; "double expressor" and "double hit" (MYC and BCL2) more aggressive
Treatment R-CHOP or Pola-R-CHP; CAR-T for early relapse; glofitamab, epcoritamab
Cure 60-70%

Treatment

  • First line: 6 cycles of R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine and prednisolone) or Pola-R-CHP (with polatuzumab vedotin, which reduced relapses in the POLARIX trial).
  • Radiotherapy for selected bulky or localised masses.
  • Central nervous system prophylaxis for high risk.
  • Relapsed or refractory:
  • Within 12 months: CAR-T (axicabtagene ciloleucel or lisocabtagene), superior to autologous transplant (ZUMA-7 and TRANSFORM trials).
  • Later relapse: salvage chemotherapy and autologous transplant.
  • Bispecific antibodies (glofitamab, epcoritamab), tafasitamab with lenalidomide, loncastuximab.

See also: Lymphomas, Primary CNS lymphoma, Stem cell transplant and CAR-T.
