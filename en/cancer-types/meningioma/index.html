Cancer types

Meningioma

The most common primary brain tumour. It arises from the meninges and is almost always benign; many need only monitoring.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common Over a third of primary nervous system tumours
Who More in women (2-3 to 1) and over 50
WHO grades 1 (80%), 2 atypical (15-20%), 3 anaplastic (1-2%)
Treatment Monitoring, surgery or radiosurgery

What it is

The meninges are the membranes covering the brain and spinal cord. Meningioma grows from them and presses on the brain without infiltrating it. Many are found by chance on MRI.

Risk factors: radiation to the head, neurofibromatosis type 2, hormones (some progestogens such as cyproterone acetate, nomegestrol acetate and chlormadinone acetate are linked to meningiomas, so European medicines agencies restricted their use from 2018-2022).

Symptoms

They depend on location: headache, seizures, weakness, visual or smell disturbances, behaviour changes.

Treatment

  • Monitoring with MRI for small tumours without symptoms.
  • Surgery removing it with its dural base: cures most grade 1 tumours.
  • Radiosurgery (one session) for small tumours in difficult places, or fractionated radiotherapy for grades 2-3 or residual tumour.
  • If linked to progestogens, stopping the drug may shrink it.

Outlook

Excellent for grade 1; grade 2-3 tumours recur more often.

See also: Brain tumours, Pituitary tumours.
