## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 2-3 cases per 100,000 a year |
| **Who** | Two peaks: 15-35 years and over 55 |
| **Typical cell** | **Reed-Sternberg cell** (CD30+, CD15+) |
| **Signs** | Painless node in the neck or chest, B symptoms, itching |
| **Treatment** | PET-adapted ABVD or BEACOPP (BrECADD); radiotherapy; brentuximab, nivolumab |
| **Cure** | Over 90% in early stages; 85% in advanced |

## What it is

Described by Thomas Hodgkin in 1832. Its malignant **Reed-Sternberg** cells are few, surrounded by many normal inflammatory cells. Epstein-Barr virus plays a part in some cases.

## Treatment

- **Localised stages**: 2-4 cycles of **ABVD** with or without involved-site **radiotherapy**, depending on the interim **PET**.
- **Advanced**: **BrECADD** (a brentuximab regimen that in the HD21 trial was more effective and less toxic than escalated BEACOPP) or PET-adapted ABVD; in the USA, **nivolumab with AVD** (S1826 trial).
- **Relapse**: salvage chemotherapy and **autologous transplant**; brentuximab, pembrolizumab or nivolumab.

## Long-term effects

Because young people are cured, late effects matter: **second cancers** (breast cancer in women given chest radiotherapy), heart disease, underactive thyroid, infertility. Treatment is therefore adapted to use the lowest effective intensity, and survivors need follow-up (for example, early breast screening after chest radiotherapy).
