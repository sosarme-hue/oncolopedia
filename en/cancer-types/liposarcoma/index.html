Cancer types

Liposarcoma

The most common soft tissue sarcoma in adults, arising from fat cells. Its subtypes range from very slow to aggressive.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common 15-20% of soft tissue sarcomas
Where Thigh and retroperitoneum
Subtypes Well-differentiated/dedifferentiated (MDM2-amplified), myxoid (FUS-DDIT3), pleomorphic
Treatment Surgery; radiotherapy (very effective in myxoid); trabectedin, eribulin

Subtypes

  • Well-differentiated (atypical lipomatous tumour in the limbs): does not spread but recurs locally.
  • Dedifferentiated: more aggressive; common in the retroperitoneum.
  • Myxoid: in young people; very sensitive to radiotherapy and trabectedin; may spread to unusual sites (spine, abdomen).
  • Pleomorphic: the most aggressive, rare.

Treatment

Wide surgery (in the retroperitoneum, often with neighbouring organs); radiotherapy before surgery for myxoid; in advanced disease, doxorubicin, trabectedin or eribulin; MDM2 and CDK4 inhibitors under study.

See also: Sarcomas, Leiomyosarcoma.
