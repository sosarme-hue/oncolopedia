Hormone therapy

Some cancers are driven by hormones. Blocking them is one of the most effective treatments for breast and prostate cancer.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

What it is

Some cancers use the body's hormones as fuel to grow: oestrogens in many breast and endometrial cancers, and androgens (such as testosterone) in prostate cancer. Hormone therapy (or endocrine therapy) reduces production of those hormones or blocks their action. It has nothing to do with menopause hormone treatment, and it is not chemotherapy.

In breast cancer

It is used for hormone receptor-positive tumours, about two in three:

  • Tamoxifen: blocks the oestrogen receptor. Used before and after menopause.
  • Aromatase inhibitors (letrozole, anastrozole, exemestane): reduce oestrogen production after menopause.
  • Ovarian suppression in higher-risk premenopausal women.
  • Other drugs (fulvestrant, elacestrant) in advanced disease, often with CDK4/6 inhibitors.

After surgery it is taken for 5 to 10 years and greatly reduces the risk of relapse. The most common effects are hot flushes, joint pain, vaginal dryness and, with aromatase inhibitors, bone loss. If they are troublesome, say so: there are solutions, and stopping treatment on your own increases the risk.

In prostate cancer

Androgen deprivation therapy lowers testosterone with regular injections (LHRH agonists or antagonists) or, less often, surgery. It is used with radiotherapy for intermediate or high-risk tumours, and in advanced disease. Newer antiandrogens (abiraterone, enzalutamide, apalutamide, darolutamide) have extended survival.

Its effects include hot flushes, tiredness, loss of sex drive and of muscle and bone mass, and increased cardiovascular and metabolic risk. Exercise helps counter them.

Other uses

In some endometrial cancers, in neuroendocrine tumours (somatostatin analogues) and, after thyroidectomy, thyroid hormone at doses that slow the growth of thyroid tissue.

See also: Breast cancer, Prostate cancer.
