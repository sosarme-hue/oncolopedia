## A hen with a tumour

In 1909 a farmer brought a Plymouth Rock hen with a lump on its breast to the Rockefeller Institute in New York. The young pathologist Peyton Rous found it was a **sarcoma**. He ground it up, passed it through a filter so fine that it held back cells and bacteria, and injected the fluid into other hens. They developed the same tumour.

## A filterable agent

In 1911 he reported that the chicken cancer was transmitted by an agent smaller than a bacterium: a **virus**. It is now called **Rous sarcoma virus**. Scientists received it sceptically, because no similar viruses were found in mammals, and Rous left the field for years.

## Recognised half a century later

Decades later the Rous virus led to the discovery of the first **oncogene**, *src*, and to the understanding that oncogenes are altered versions of normal genes in our cells. We now know several viruses cause cancer in humans: **papillomavirus** (HPV), **hepatitis B and C**, **Epstein-Barr virus** and **HTLV-1**. In 1966, aged 87, Rous received the Nobel Prize, one of the longest gaps between a discovery and its recognition.
