## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 3-7% of uterine cancers |
| **Types** | Leiomyosarcoma, low- and high-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma, adenosarcoma |
| **Symptoms** | Abnormal bleeding, pain, a womb that grows quickly |
| **Difficulty** | It is hard to tell from a fibroid before surgery |
| **Treatment** | Complete hysterectomy without fragmenting the womb |

## What it is

**Uterine sarcomas** arise in the **myometrium** (the muscle of the womb) or the endometrial **stroma**. They are rare and very different from each other:

- **Leiomyosarcoma**: the most common; aggressive, tending to recur in lung and liver.
- **Low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma**: slow-growing, hormone receptor-positive, late relapses.
- **High-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma** and undifferentiated sarcoma: aggressive.
- **Adenosarcoma**: a mix of benign glands and malignant stroma.

**Carcinosarcoma** is now considered a high-risk endometrial carcinoma, not a sarcoma.

## Risk factors

Age (mainly around and after menopause), previous pelvic radiotherapy, tamoxifen treatment and some inherited syndromes (such as hereditary retinoblastoma or *FH*-related syndrome).

## The fibroid problem

**Fibroids** are very common and benign, and a leiomyosarcoma can look like a fibroid on ultrasound. No imaging test rules it out completely, although **MRI** helps. That is why health agencies advise against unprotected **morcellation** (cutting up the womb inside the abdomen to remove it by keyhole surgery) in older women or when there is suspicion, because it can spread a hidden sarcoma.

## Diagnosis and treatment

- MRI and sometimes biopsy; often the diagnosis comes after surgery.
- **Total hysterectomy en bloc**, without fragmentation. In low-grade stromal sarcoma the ovaries are also removed because of hormone sensitivity.
- After surgery for localised leiomyosarcoma, chemotherapy or radiotherapy have not been shown to improve survival; decided case by case.
- Advanced disease: **chemotherapy** (doxorubicin, gemcitabine with docetaxel, trabectedin), **hormone therapy** (aromatase inhibitors) for low-grade stromal sarcoma, and surgery for selected metastases.

## Outlook

It varies widely: low-grade stromal sarcoma has five-year survival above 90% at stage I, while leiomyosarcoma, even when localised, relapses in more than half of cases. Treatment in **sarcoma reference centres** is advisable.
