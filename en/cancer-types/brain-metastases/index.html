Cancer types

Brain metastases

The most common brain tumours, coming from lung, breast, melanoma or kidney cancers. Radiosurgery and drugs that cross the blood-brain barrier have greatly improved their control.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common 10-30% of people with cancer; 10 times more common than primary brain tumours
Origin Lung (50%), breast (15-20%), melanoma, kidney, bowel
Treatment Radiosurgery, surgery, systemic drugs active in the brain; whole-brain radiotherapy in selected cases

What they are

Cancer cells that reach the brain through the blood. They were once considered a terminal stage; today many patients control them for years.

Symptoms

Headache, seizures, weakness, confusion, problems with balance or speech. Sometimes found on a routine MRI.

Treatment

  • Stereotactic radiosurgery for one or several metastases (even up to 10-15), sparing the rest of the brain and protecting memory.
  • Surgery for large, single or symptomatic metastases, followed by radiosurgery to the cavity.
  • Whole-brain radiotherapy with hippocampal sparing and memantine, for very extensive disease.
  • Drugs that cross the blood-brain barrier: osimertinib and lorlatinib (lung EGFR and ALK), tucatinib and trastuzumab deruxtecan (HER2 breast), nivolumab with ipilimumab (melanoma).
  • Dexamethasone for swelling; anti-seizure drugs only if seizures have occurred.

See also: Brain tumours, Lung cancer, HER2-positive breast cancer, Melanoma.
