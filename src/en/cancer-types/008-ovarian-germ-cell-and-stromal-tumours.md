## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | About 5-10% of malignant ovarian tumours |
| **Who** | Germ cell tumours mainly in girls, teenagers and women under 30; stromal tumours at any age |
| **Main types** | Dysgerminoma, yolk sac tumour, immature teratoma, granulosa cell tumour |
| **Markers** | Alpha-fetoprotein, beta-hCG, LDH; inhibin in granulosa cell tumours |
| **Outlook** | Germ cell tumours are cured in over 90% of cases, even when advanced |

## What they are

The ovary has three kinds of cells that can give rise to tumours: those covering it (the origin of carcinomas), **germ cells** (which give rise to eggs) and **stromal and sex cord** cells, which make hormones.

**Malignant germ cell tumours**

- **Dysgerminoma**: the most common; equivalent to testicular seminoma. Very sensitive to chemotherapy and radiotherapy.
- **Yolk sac tumour**: produces alpha-fetoprotein; grows quickly.
- **Immature teratoma**: contains immature embryonic tissues. Mature teratoma (dermoid cyst) is benign and very common.
- Embryonal carcinoma, non-gestational choriocarcinoma and mixed tumours, all rare.

**Sex cord-stromal tumours**

- **Adult granulosa cell tumour**: produces oestrogen, which can cause bleeding after menopause or a thickened womb lining. It almost always has the *FOXL2* mutation and tends to relapse late, even decades later.
- Juvenile granulosa cell tumour, Sertoli-Leydig cell tumour (can produce androgens and cause masculine features) and others.

## Symptoms

- **Abdominal pain**, sometimes sudden from twisting or rupture of the tumour.
- A **mass** or swollen abdomen, as they can be large.
- Hormonal changes: early puberty in girls, irregular bleeding, excess hair or a deeper voice.

## Diagnosis

Ultrasound, MRI or CT, and blood **tumour markers** (alpha-fetoprotein, beta-hCG, LDH, inhibin, AMH). The final diagnosis comes from examining the removed tumour. Girls and teenagers are treated in paediatric oncology units.

## Treatment

- **Fertility-sparing surgery**: usually only the affected ovary is removed, keeping the womb and other ovary, with staging.
- **BEP chemotherapy** (bleomycin, etoposide and cisplatin) for germ cell tumours, except very early low-risk stages, where surveillance is enough.
- For granulosa cell tumours, surgery and, in advanced stages or relapse, platinum-based chemotherapy, further surgery or hormone therapy.

## Outlook and follow-up

Malignant germ cell tumours have an excellent outlook: over 90% are cured, and most women keep the ability to have children. Follow-up includes markers and imaging. Granulosa cell tumours need very long follow-up because of late relapses.
