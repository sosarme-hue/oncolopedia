Merkel cell carcinoma

A rare, aggressive skin cancer caused by a polyomavirus or by sun damage. It was one of the first tumours to respond to immunotherapy: in advanced disease about half of patients respond, and some responses last.

At a glance
How common Rare: under 1 case per 100,000 people a year, rising
Who Over-70s, fair skin, people with weakened immunity
Causes Merkel cell polyomavirus (80%) or ultraviolet damage
Appearance A painless red-violet lump growing quickly on exposed skin
Treatment Surgery with sentinel node and radiotherapy; immunotherapy (avelumab, pembrolizumab)

What it is

It is a neuroendocrine carcinoma of the skin. The acronym AEIOU helps: Asymptomatic, Expanding rapidly, Immunosuppression, Older than 50, UV-exposed site. It tends to spread to nodes and recur.

Diagnosis

Biopsy with immunohistochemistry (dot-like CK20), sentinel node, PET-CT, and blood antibodies against the polyomavirus, useful during follow-up.

Treatment

  • Surgery with margins and sentinel node biopsy.
  • Radiotherapy to the site and nodes, as the tumour is very radiosensitive.
  • Advanced disease: avelumab, pembrolizumab or retifanlimab, with responses in half of patients, many lasting. Chemotherapy works, but briefly.
  • Adjuvant immunotherapy is being studied.

Outlook

Five-year survival is 50-70% for localised disease; worse with nodes or metastases, although immunotherapy has greatly improved the figures.

See also: Melanoma, Gastrointestinal neuroendocrine tumours, Immunotherapy.
