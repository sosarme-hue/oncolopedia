## Against radical mastectomy

For almost a century breast cancer was treated with the **Halsted radical mastectomy**, which removed the breast, chest muscles and armpit lymph nodes. It was based on the idea that cancer spread in an orderly way and that the more removed, the better.

Bernard Fisher, a surgeon at the University of Pittsburgh, proposed another hypothesis: breast cancer may be a **systemic disease** from the start, with cells travelling in the blood, and the extent of local surgery does not by itself determine survival.

## The NSABP trials

Leading the **NSABP** cooperative group, he organised large **randomised clinical trials**, something unusual in surgery at the time:

- **B-04** showed that radical mastectomy did not improve survival compared with less extensive surgery.
- **B-06**, published in 1985, showed that **lumpectomy with radiotherapy** gave the same survival as mastectomy. After 20 years, the results held.
- His trials also drove **adjuvant chemotherapy and hormone therapy** and prevention with tamoxifen in high-risk women.

## His legacy

Fisher changed breast cancer surgery and argued that medical decisions should rest on trials, not authority. In the 1990s he faced a bitter controversy over data falsified by an investigator at one participating centre; later analyses confirmed his conclusions did not change.
