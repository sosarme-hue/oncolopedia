## How it works

Radiotherapy uses **ionising radiation** to damage the DNA of cancer cells, which find it harder to repair than normal cells do. It is aimed at a specific area of the body, so its effects are mostly limited to that area. About half of all cancer patients are estimated to need it at some point.

## What it is used for

- **Radical**: as the main curative treatment, for example in head and neck, cervical, prostate or inoperable lung cancer.
- **Adjuvant**: after surgery, to reduce the risk of local relapse, as after breast-conserving surgery.
- **Neoadjuvant**: before surgery, as in rectal cancer.
- **Palliative**: to relieve pain from bone metastases, bleeding or compression, often in a few sessions.

## Types

- **External beam radiotherapy**: a linear accelerator directs beams from outside. Techniques such as **IMRT**, **VMAT** and image-guided radiotherapy shape the dose to the tumour and protect nearby organs.
- **Stereotactic radiotherapy (SBRT, radiosurgery)**: very high, precise doses in a few sessions, for small tumours of the lung, brain or liver, or limited metastases.
- **Brachytherapy**: the radioactive source is placed inside or next to the tumour, as in cervical or prostate cancer. It is the direct descendant of the radium therapy of the early 20th century.
- **Proton beam therapy**: uses protons, which deposit their energy at a set depth. Mainly useful in children and for tumours near delicate structures.
- **Radiopharmaceuticals**: radioactive substances injected that seek out tumour cells, such as radioactive iodine for thyroid cancer or lutetium-PSMA for prostate cancer.

## What treatment is like

First a **planning scan** is done. Sessions are then usually daily, Monday to Friday, for a few days or several weeks, and last a few minutes. It does not hurt and does not make the patient radioactive (except with some internal sources, for a while).

## Side effects

They depend on the area treated: tiredness, skin irritation, a sore mouth or oesophagus, diarrhoea, discomfort passing urine. They usually appear during treatment and improve weeks later. Some late effects appear months or years later, which is why there is follow-up.
