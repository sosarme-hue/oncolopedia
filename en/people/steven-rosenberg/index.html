Steven Rosenberg

The US National Cancer Institute surgeon who showed that the immune system can eliminate advanced tumours. A pioneer of cell therapy.

An unforgettable patient

In 1968, as a resident, Steven Rosenberg met a man whose metastatic stomach cancer had disappeared without treatment twelve years earlier. Rosenberg became convinced the immune system had eliminated it and devoted his career to trying to reproduce that.

Interleukin-2

In 1974 he became chief of surgery at the National Cancer Institute (NCI). In 1985 he reported that interleukin-2, a substance that stimulates T lymphocytes, produced regressions of metastatic melanoma and kidney cancer. Responses occurred only in a minority, but some were complete and lasting: patients still alive decades later. In 1992 it became the first immune treatment approved for an advanced cancer.

Lymphocytes from the tumour itself

His team took from tumours the tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) that already recognised the cancer, grew them by billions in the laboratory and returned them to the patient. In 1990 he carried out the first authorised gene therapy in humans, with marked lymphocytes. This work paved the way for CAR-T cells and TIL therapy, approved for melanoma in 2024.

See also: Immunotherapy, Stem cell transplant and CAR-T, Carl June.
