## Not every symptom can wait

Most of these symptoms are **not caused by cancer**. But they are not all equally urgent: some need help the same day, others an appointment within a few days even if they happened only once, and only the mildest justify waiting to see whether they persist. This page is for guidance and **does not replace a professional assessment**: if in doubt, call your doctor or the emergency number (**112** in Europe, **999** in the UK, **911** in the US).

Do not wait for your next screening round: screening is for people without symptoms.

## 1. Get help straight away: emergency department or emergency number

Go to the emergency department or call the emergency number if any of these appear, especially suddenly or severely:

- **Vomiting blood**, black tarry stools or **heavy bleeding** from anywhere, especially with dizziness, pallor or fainting.
- **Coughing up a lot of blood** or sudden **shortness of breath**.
- **Weakness** or numbness in an arm or leg, difficulty speaking, confusion or a **first seizure**.
- A **sudden, very severe headache**, or a headache with vomiting that is quickly getting worse.
- New **back pain** with weakness in the legs, or loss of bladder or bowel control.
- High **fever** with yellow skin, or fever in someone having chemotherapy.

## 2. See a doctor soon, within days, even if it happened only once

These are not usually emergencies, but **do not wait for them to happen again**. Book an appointment with your GP as soon as you can:

| Symptom | Can be linked to |
|---|---|
| **Blood in your urine**, even once and without pain | Bladder, kidney |
| **Blood in your stools** or black stools | Bowel, upper digestive tract |
| **Vaginal bleeding** after the menopause, between periods or after sex | Womb, cervix |
| A new **lump** in the breast, testicle, neck or armpit | Breast, testicle, lymphomas, sarcomas and others |
| **Coughing up blood**, even a little | Lung |
| **Difficulty swallowing** or food feeling stuck | Oesophagus, stomach |
| **Jaundice** (yellow skin or eyes) | Pancreas, bile ducts, liver |
| A **mole that changes** size, shape or colour, or bleeds | Melanoma |
| Unexplained **bruising**, bleeding or frequent infections | Leukaemias |
| Significant **weight loss** for no reason | Many cancers |
| A new, worsening **headache**, especially with vomiting or changes in vision | Brain tumours |

## 3. Milder symptoms: see a doctor if they last more than two or three weeks or get worse

These are very common and almost always benign. Get them checked if they **do not go away** within two or three weeks, if they **get worse** or if they come with any of the symptoms above:

| Symptom | Can be linked to |
|---|---|
| A **cough** or **hoarseness** lasting more than three weeks | Lung, larynx |
| A **mouth ulcer** or skin sore that has not healed in three weeks | Mouth cancer, skin cancer |
| An unexplained **change in bowel habit** (diarrhoea or constipation) | Bowel |
| Persistent **bloating** and feeling full quickly | Ovary |
| **Indigestion** or heartburn that does not improve with treatment | Stomach, oesophagus |
| Severe **tiredness**, unexplained **fever** or **night sweats** | Lymphomas and others |
| Unexplained **pain** in a bone or one particular place that does not go away | Bone, metastases |

## In children

Cancer in children is rare, but **see a doctor soon** for pallor and bruising without knocks, prolonged fever, bone pain that wakes them at night, limping, morning headaches with vomiting, a lump in the abdomen or a **white reflection in the pupil** in photos. If the child is listless, very pale or bleeding, go to the emergency department.

## When you see a doctor

Explain when it started, how it has changed and whether there is a family history. If the symptom continues or worsens despite a first treatment, go back even if you have already been seen. If you are already being treated for cancer, follow your team’s advice on when to call them. Many health systems have **fast-track pathways** for suspected cancer.
