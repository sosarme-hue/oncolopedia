## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Types** | Basal cell carcinoma (the most common), squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma |
| **Main cause** | Ultraviolet radiation from the sun and sunbeds |
| **Typical sign** | A mole that changes, or a sore or spot that does not heal |
| **ABCDE rule** | Asymmetry, irregular Borders, varied Colour, Diameter over 6 mm, Evolution |
| **Melanoma survival** | Over 85% at five years in Europe; over 95% for thin melanomas |

## What it is

- **Basal cell carcinoma** is the most common cancer in fair-skinned people. It grows slowly and almost never spreads, but can destroy tissue if left untreated.
- **Squamous cell carcinoma** is the second most common. It appears on sun-exposed skin, often on precancerous patches called **actinic keratoses**, and spreads to lymph nodes more often than basal cell carcinoma.
- **Melanoma** starts in melanocytes, the cells that give skin its colour. It is less common but causes most skin cancer deaths because it can spread early. It can also arise in mucous membranes and the eye.

## Risk factors

Sun exposure, especially **sunburn in childhood**, sunbeds (classified as carcinogenic by IARC), fair skin that burns easily, many moles or atypical moles, family history and a weakened immune system (for example after a transplant).

## Symptoms

- A new mole in adulthood, or one that changes shape, colour or size, itches or bleeds.
- The "ugly duckling" mole: different from the person's other moles.
- A pearly bump, a scab or a sore that has not healed in weeks.

## Diagnosis

A dermatologist examines the skin with a **dermatoscope**. If suspicious, the lesion is removed and examined. In melanoma, **Breslow thickness** and ulceration are the most important findings, and they determine whether a **sentinel lymph node biopsy** is done.

## Treatment

- Basal and squamous cell carcinoma: **surgery** is almost always curative. Other options (creams, cryotherapy, photodynamic therapy, radiotherapy) exist for superficial lesions or people who cannot have surgery, and drugs for rare advanced cases.
- Localised melanoma: **surgery** with margins. If the risk of relapse is high, adjuvant immunotherapy.
- Advanced melanoma: **immunotherapy** with anti-PD-1, alone or with anti-CTLA-4, and **targeted therapy** against *BRAF* and *MEK* if the tumour carries the mutation (about half). Before 2011, survival was measured in months; today a substantial share of patients live for many years.

## Prevention

Avoid the midday sun, wear clothing, a hat and sunglasses, use broad-spectrum sunscreen, avoid sunbeds and **protect children in particular**. Check your skin and see a doctor about any change.
