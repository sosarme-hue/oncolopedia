## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 74,000 cases a year worldwide; about 2,400 in the UK |
| **Who** | Men aged 15 to 40 |
| **Types** | Seminoma (50%) and non-seminoma (embryonal, yolk sac, choriocarcinoma, teratoma) |
| **Sign** | **Painless lump or hardening** in a testicle |
| **Markers** | Blood AFP, beta-hCG and LDH |
| **Treatment** | Orchidectomy; surveillance, chemotherapy (BEP) or radiotherapy as needed |
| **Cure** | Over 95% overall; 80% even with extensive metastases |

## What it is

Over 95% are **germ cell tumours**, arising from the cells that should form sperm. Almost all come from a precursor lesion, **germ cell neoplasia *in situ***, which forms during fetal development.

- **Seminoma**: slower growing, average age 35, very sensitive to radiotherapy and chemotherapy.
- **Non-seminoma**: average age 25-30; more aggressive but also very curable. Many are **mixed**.

Rare others: Leydig or Sertoli cell tumours, and lymphomas in older men.

## Risk factors

- **Undescended testicle** (cryptorchidism), even if corrected.
- **Family history** (father or brother).
- Previous testicular cancer (risk in the other testicle).
- Infertility, Klinefelter syndrome, HIV.
- More common in white men and in northern Europe.

## Symptoms and self-examination

- A **lump or hardening** of a testicle, usually painless.
- Heaviness in the scrotum, dull ache in the lower abdomen.
- Breast enlargement (from hCG).
- With metastases: back pain, cough, breathlessness.

**Monthly self-examination**, after a warm shower: roll each testicle between the fingers feeling for lumps or changes in size or firmness. Any change lasting more than two weeks should be checked.

## Diagnosis

1. **Scrotal ultrasound**: the key test.
2. **Tumour markers**: **AFP** (raised in non-seminomas with yolk sac), **beta-hCG** and **LDH**.
3. Inguinal **orchidectomy**: the testicle is removed through the groin (no biopsy through the scrotum). A prosthesis can be fitted.
4. **CT** of chest, abdomen and pelvis.
5. **Sperm banking** before chemotherapy or radiotherapy.

## Treatment

- **Stage I** (no metastases): most need only **surveillance** with CT and markers; alternatively, one cycle of chemotherapy (carboplatin for seminoma, BEP for non-seminoma) to reduce relapse risk.
- **Metastatic disease**: **BEP** chemotherapy (bleomycin, etoposide and cisplatin) 3-4 cycles depending on risk (IGCCCG classification). For seminomas with few nodes, radiotherapy or retroperitoneal node surgery.
- **Surgery for residual masses** after chemotherapy, in expert centres.
- **Relapse**: salvage chemotherapy, including high-dose with stem cell support.

## Outlook

It is one of oncology's great successes: before **cisplatin** (1970s, work of Lawrence Einhorn) metastatic disease was almost always fatal; today over 95% are cured. Cyclist **Lance Armstrong** was cured of testicular cancer with brain and lung metastases in 1996.

## After treatment

- Follow-up with CT and markers for 5-10 years.
- Late effects to watch: **cardiovascular risk**, high blood pressure, cholesterol, low testosterone, hearing problems, neuropathy, second cancers and psychological effects.
- **Fertility** is preserved in many men with one testicle; otherwise frozen sperm is used.
