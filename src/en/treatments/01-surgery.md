## What it is for

Surgery is the oldest cancer treatment and still the one that cures the most localised solid tumours. It can have different aims:

- **Diagnosis**: obtaining a biopsy when it cannot be done another way.
- **Cure**: removing the whole tumour with a margin of healthy tissue and the nearby lymph nodes.
- **Staging**: finding out how far the cancer has really spread.
- **Debulking**: removing as much tumour as possible, as in ovarian cancer.
- **Metastases**: removing selected metastases, for example in the liver or lung in colorectal cancer.
- **Palliation**: relieving a blockage, bleeding or pain.
- **Prevention**: in people at very high inherited risk.
- **Reconstruction**: after a mastectomy or head and neck surgery.

## Margins and lymph nodes

The pathologist examines the removed tissue to check that the **edges (margins)** are free of cancer. An involved margin may need further surgery or added radiotherapy.

The **sentinel lymph node** technique uses a tracer to find the first node the tumour drains to. If it is free of cancer, the remaining nodes in the area do not need to be removed, which reduces after-effects such as lymphoedema of the arm. It is used in breast cancer, melanoma, endometrial cancer and others.

## Less aggressive, just as effective

Over the last century cancer surgery has moved from "the more, the better" to "what is necessary". Halsted's radical mastectomy gave way to breast-conserving surgery with radiotherapy after Bernard Fisher's trials. Today **keyhole (laparoscopic)** and **robotic surgery** allow operations through small incisions, with less pain and quicker recovery, and **enhanced recovery programmes** prepare patients before the operation.

## Before and after

Many operations are combined with **neoadjuvant** treatment (before, to shrink the tumour) or **adjuvant** treatment (after, to reduce the risk of relapse). Complex operations, such as on the pancreas, oesophagus, liver or sarcomas, have better results in **high-volume centres**.

Before surgery, ask about its aim, the alternatives, possible after-effects and what recovery will be like. Stopping smoking, walking and eating well before the operation all help.
