## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | Stomach: 970,000 cases a year worldwide; oesophagus: 510,000 |
| **Causes** | *Helicobacter pylori*, tobacco, alcohol, reflux and obesity, a diet high in salt and smoked foods |
| **Signs** | Difficulty swallowing, persistent indigestion, weight loss, anaemia, vomiting |
| **Diagnosis** | Endoscopy with biopsy |
| **Treatment** | Surgery with chemotherapy before and after; chemoradiotherapy for the oesophagus; immunotherapy and targeted therapy |
| **Survival** | Varies widely by stage; see "Outlook" and the page for each organ |

## What they are

The **oesophagus** (gullet) and **stomach** make up the upper digestive tract. Their cancers are considered together because tumours at the **junction between them** (gastro-oesophageal junction) are increasingly common and treated like stomach cancers.

**Oesophagus**:
- **Squamous cell carcinoma**: in the middle and upper third; linked to **tobacco and alcohol** and very hot drinks. It is the dominant type worldwide, especially in Asia and East Africa.
- **Adenocarcinoma**: in the lower third, arising from **Barrett's oesophagus** caused by chronic reflux; linked to obesity. It is the fastest-rising type in Europe and North America.

**Stomach**:
- **Adenocarcinoma** (90%), intestinal or diffuse type (with signet ring cells). Molecular classification distinguishes Epstein-Barr virus-positive, microsatellite-unstable, genomically stable and chromosomally unstable tumours.
- Others: MALT lymphomas (linked to *H. pylori*), [GIST](../gastrointestinal-stromal-tumour-gist/) and [neuroendocrine tumours](../gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumours/).

## How common they are

Stomach cancer has fallen greatly in Europe thanks to refrigeration (less salted food) and declining *H. pylori*, but remains very common in East Asia, Eastern Europe and Latin America. Oesophageal cancer is less common but among the deadliest; the UK has one of the highest rates of oesophageal adenocarcinoma in the world.

## Risk factors

| Factor | Stomach | Oesophagus |
|---|---|---|
| ***Helicobacter pylori*** | Main cause (chronic gastritis → atrophy → metaplasia) | Protective against adenocarcinoma |
| **Tobacco** | Yes | Yes, both types |
| **Alcohol** | Moderate | Very important for squamous |
| **Reflux and Barrett's** | At the junction | Adenocarcinoma |
| **Obesity** | At the junction | Adenocarcinoma |
| **Diet** | Salt, salted and smoked foods, little fruit | Very hot drinks, little fruit |
| **Inherited** | Hereditary diffuse gastric cancer (*CDH1*), Lynch | Tylosis (rare) |

## Prevention

- **Testing for and eradicating *H. pylori*** lowers stomach cancer risk, especially in patients' relatives. Some high-risk countries (Japan, Korea) screen with endoscopy.
- Not smoking, limiting alcohol, controlling weight and reflux.
- People with **Barrett's oesophagus** or extensive atrophic gastritis should have **regular endoscopies**.
- Carriers of *CDH1* mutations are advised to have a preventive gastrectomy.

## Symptoms

- **Dysphagia**: difficulty swallowing, first solids then liquids (typical of the oesophagus).
- Indigestion, feeling full quickly, upper abdominal pain that does not improve.
- **Weight loss**, poor appetite, nausea and vomiting.
- **Anaemia** from blood loss, black stools, vomiting blood.
- Hoarseness or coughing when eating in advanced oesophageal cancer.

Digestive complaints are very common; endoscopy is mainly indicated for **alarm symptoms** or new symptoms after age 50-55.

## Diagnosis

1. **Upper endoscopy** with biopsy.
2. **CT** of chest and abdomen, **PET-CT** for the oesophagus.
3. **Endoscopic ultrasound** to assess depth and nodes.
4. Staging **laparoscopy** for advanced stomach cancer, to look for peritoneal spread.
5. Biomarkers: **HER2**, **PD-L1** (CPS), **microsatellite instability** and **claudin 18.2**.

## Treatment

**Very early tumours** (confined to the mucosa): endoscopic removal (**submucosal dissection**), without surgery.

**Localised stomach cancer**:
- **Perioperative FLOT chemotherapy** (four cycles before and four after surgery), which improved survival in the FLOT4 trial. **Durvalumab** has recently been added (MATTERHORN trial).
- Total or partial **gastrectomy** with extended node removal (D2).

**Localised oesophageal cancer**:
- **Squamous**: **chemoradiotherapy** followed by surgery or, with a complete response, definitive chemoradiotherapy with surveillance.
- **Adenocarcinoma**: perioperative FLOT chemotherapy (preferred after the ESOPEC trial) or chemoradiotherapy (CROSS regimen) followed by surgery.
- **Oesophagectomy**, often minimally invasive, in high-volume centres.
- After chemoradiotherapy and surgery, if tumour remains, **nivolumab** for a year (CheckMate 577 trial).

**Advanced disease**:
- Platinum and fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy, plus **nivolumab or pembrolizumab** depending on PD-L1.
- **Trastuzumab** (with pembrolizumab) if HER2-positive; **trastuzumab deruxtecan** second line.
- **Zolbetuximab** if claudin 18.2 is expressed.
- Ramucirumab with paclitaxel, trifluridine-tipiracil.
- A **stent** or radiotherapy to allow swallowing; nutrition support.

## Outlook

In Europe about one in four people with stomach cancer survive five years (EUROCARE-5, diagnosed 2000-2007, relative survival). In Spain, for oesophageal cancer, 13% of men and 16% of women do (net survival, diagnosed 2008-2013, REDECAN). Tumours found very early are cured in the great majority of cases. In Japan and Korea, thanks to screening, more than half are diagnosed early.

## After treatment

After gastrectomy or oesophagectomy, **small frequent meals**, attention to protein intake and lifelong **vitamin B12** (and sometimes iron and calcium) are needed. Dumping syndrome (dizziness and sweating after eating) and reflux are common. A dietitian helps a great deal.

## Questions for your team

- Is my tumour HER2-, PD-L1- or claudin 18.2-positive?
- Will I have chemotherapy before surgery?
- How many of these operations does the hospital do each year?
- How will my eating change?
- Should my relatives be tested for *H. pylori*?
