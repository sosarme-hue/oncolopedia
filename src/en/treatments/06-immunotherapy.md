## The idea

The immune system can recognise abnormal cells and destroy them, but tumours learn to hide or to **put the brakes** on lymphocytes. **Immunotherapy** tries to restore the immune system's ability to attack cancer.

The idea is old: in the late 19th century the surgeon William Coley injected bacteria into patients with sarcoma, with inconsistent results. But modern immunotherapy began to change oncology from 2011.

## Checkpoint inhibitors

T lymphocytes have natural "brakes", such as **CTLA-4** and **PD-1**, that stop them attacking healthy tissue. Many tumours activate these brakes, for example with the protein **PD-L1**. **Checkpoint inhibitors** are antibodies that block these brakes:

- Anti-CTLA-4: ipilimumab, tremelimumab.
- Anti-PD-1: nivolumab, pembrolizumab, cemiplimab, dostarlimab.
- Anti-PD-L1: atezolizumab, durvalumab, avelumab.

James Allison and Tasuku Honjo won the 2018 Nobel Prize for the discoveries that made these drugs possible.

They are used in melanoma, lung, kidney, bladder, head and neck, liver, stomach, oesophageal, cervical and endometrial cancers, Hodgkin lymphoma, triple-negative breast cancer and tumours with **microsatellite instability**, among others. In some patients they achieve **very long-lasting responses**, even for years, which used to be exceptional in advanced disease. But they do not work for every patient or every tumour.

## Side effects

By releasing the brakes, the immune system can also attack healthy organs: **immune-related adverse events**, such as inflammation of the skin, gut (diarrhoea), liver, lungs (cough, breathlessness) or glands (thyroid, pituitary, adrenals). They can appear even months after treatment ends. Most are mild, but some are serious: **always report** new symptoms, and tell any doctor who sees you that you are having immunotherapy. They are treated with steroids and other drugs.

## Other forms of immunotherapy

- **Cell therapy**: **CAR-T** and tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes.
- **Bispecific antibodies**, which bring T cells close to tumour cells.
- **BCG** into the bladder in bladder cancer.
- **Therapeutic vaccines**, in research, including personalised mRNA vaccines.
