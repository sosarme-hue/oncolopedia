Harald zur Hausen

The German virologist who showed that human papillomavirus causes cervical cancer. His discovery made a vaccine against cancer possible.

Against the tide

In the 1970s cervical cancer was thought to be caused by herpes simplex virus. Harald zur Hausen could find no trace of herpes in the tumours and suspected another virus: the papillomavirus, which causes warts. For ten years he searched for its DNA in cancer biopsies while many colleagues doubted him.

HPV 16 and 18

In 1983 and 1984 his team in Germany isolated two new types of human papillomavirus, HPV 16 and HPV 18, in cervical cancer samples. We now know they are present in about 70% of these cancers, and that HPV causes virtually all of them, as well as some cancers of the anus, vulva, vagina, penis and oropharynx.

From a virus to a vaccine

If a virus causes cancer, a vaccine can prevent it. Zur Hausen argued for the idea in the face of an industry slow to take interest. The first HPV vaccines were approved in 2006, and in countries with high coverage cervical cancer is falling markedly. In 2008 he received the Nobel Prize in Medicine. He led the German Cancer Research Center (DKFZ) in Heidelberg for 20 years.
