## Sources in every article

Every article cites its sources: reference bodies such as the World Health Organization and its International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), the US National Cancer Institute, health ministries, scientific societies (ESMO, ASCO, SEOM and others), patient organisations, and peer-reviewed research, above all clinical trials and guidelines.

## Proven, under study, or for sale

We clearly separate what is already part of standard care, what is still being researched and what is sold without evidence. A promising headline is not an available treatment.

## Figures in context

We give incidence, survival and risk figures with their source and context, and we remember that a statistic describes a group, not one person's future.

## Respect for people

We avoid battle language that blames those who "lose the fight", and expressions such as "suffers from" or "victim". Nobody is to blame for their cancer. We include patient organisations as a source of support.

## We do not replace your care team

Oncolopedia cannot be used to choose a treatment. We want to help you understand and ask better questions.

## Review procedure {#procedure}

**Who writes.** The texts are written by Alejandro Rosàs, a family physician and specialist in Preventive Medicine and Public Health, from the sources cited in each article.

**Specialist review.** For now the fact sheets have **no external review** by an oncologist or other specialist. When a page has one, the reviewer's name and specialty will appear at the foot of the article and in the page data. If a page shows no reviewer, it has not had one.

**How often each topic is reviewed.**

| Content | Scheduled review | Immediate review |
|---|---|---|
| Screening, prevention and symptoms | Every **6 months** | When an official rule or programme changes |
| Cancer types, diagnosis and treatments | At least **once a year** | When a guideline (ESMO, NCCN, NICE) or a drug approval changes practice |
| Living with cancer and FAQ | Once a year | When a resource or a right changes |
| History, people and glossary | Every two years | If an error is found |

Clinical errors reported by readers are reviewed within **seven days**.

**Two kinds of change.** Each article shows two dates, because correcting a medical fact is not the same as changing the design:

- **Clinical correction**: changes a figure, a recommendation, an indication, a timeframe or anything that could influence a decision. It updates the "**Clinical content reviewed**" date and is logged below.
- **Presentation change**: design, links, image credits, table of contents or wording without changing the content. It only updates the "**Updated**" date.

If you find a mistake or something out of date, write to [partenopides@gmail.com](mailto:partenopides@gmail.com).
