## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 950,000 cases a year worldwide (seventh most common); about 12,800 in the UK |
| **Type** | Squamous cell carcinoma (90%) |
| **Causes** | **Tobacco and alcohol** (together multiply risk), **HPV** (oropharynx), Epstein-Barr virus (nasopharynx), betel nut |
| **Signs** | A mouth ulcer not healing after 3 weeks, persistent hoarseness, a neck lump, painful swallowing |
| **Treatment** | Surgery, radiotherapy, chemoradiotherapy, immunotherapy |
| **Survival** | 80-90% in early stages; 40-60% in advanced (better if HPV-positive) |

The **thyroid**, although in the neck, is considered separately: see [thyroid cancer](../thyroid-cancer/).

## Risk factors

- **Tobacco** (cigarettes, cigars, pipe, chewing) and **alcohol**: each multiplies risk, and together up to 30 times.
- **HPV 16**: causes most tonsil and tongue base cancers in Europe and North America, in younger people often non-smokers. It is linked to oral sex.
- **Epstein-Barr** virus (nasopharynx), **betel nut** and chewing tobacco (South Asia), poor dental hygiene, workplace exposures (wood dust, nickel, formaldehyde for sinuses), weakened immunity, Fanconi anaemia.

## Prevention

Not smoking, limiting alcohol, **HPV vaccination** (girls and boys), dental hygiene and mouth checks by the dentist.

## Warning symptoms (over 3 weeks)

- A mouth or tongue **ulcer** that does not heal; white (**leukoplakia**) or red (**erythroplakia**) patches.
- Persistent **hoarseness**.
- A painless **neck lump**.
- Pain or difficulty swallowing, feeling of something in the throat, one-sided earache.
- One-sided blocked nose, nosebleeds, hearing loss in one ear (nasopharynx).

## Diagnosis

Examination of the mouth and neck, **nasendoscopy** (a thin camera through the nose), **biopsy**, ultrasound-guided needle sampling of nodes, **CT and/or MRI**, **PET-CT** for advanced stages. For the oropharynx, **HPV (p16)** testing, which changes stage and outlook.

## Treatment

It is decided by a **multidisciplinary team** (ENT and maxillofacial surgeons, clinical and medical oncologists, speech and language therapist, dietitian, dentist).

- **Early stages** (60% cured with a single treatment): **surgery** (including transoral laser or robotic) or **radiotherapy**.
- **Advanced stages**: surgery with reconstruction (free microvascular flaps) followed by radiotherapy or chemoradiotherapy; or **cisplatin chemoradiotherapy** as the main treatment to **preserve the organ** (larynx, pharynx).
- **Pembrolizumab** before and after surgery for advanced resectable tumours (KEYNOTE-689 trial, 2025).
- **Recurrent or metastatic**: **pembrolizumab** alone or with chemotherapy (KEYNOTE-048), **cetuximab**, salvage surgery or re-irradiation.

## Effects and rehabilitation

- **Dry mouth** (xerostomia), taste changes, mucositis, swallowing problems (dysphagia), voice changes.
- **Dental assessment** before radiotherapy (to avoid osteoradionecrosis) and lifelong fluoride.
- **Speech and language therapy** for voice and swallowing; sometimes a temporary feeding tube.
- **Intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT)** and proton therapy reduce dry mouth.
- Underactive thyroid after neck radiotherapy: regular blood tests.
- Psychological support: appearance and communication are affected.

## Outlook

HPV-positive oropharyngeal tumours have the best outlook (over 80% at five years). **Stopping smoking and drinking** improves treatment effectiveness and reduces second tumours, common in this area and in the lung and oesophagus.

## Questions for your team

- Is my tumour HPV-positive?
- Which options best preserve voice and swallowing?
- Should I see a dentist before starting?
- Who will help me eat and speak afterwards?
