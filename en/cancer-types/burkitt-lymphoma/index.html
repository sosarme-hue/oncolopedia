Cancer types

Burkitt lymphoma

The fastest-growing human tumour, doubling in size in a day. Even so, with intensive chemotherapy about 90% of children and most adults, especially younger ones, are cured.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
Alteration MYC translocation (t(8;14))
Forms Endemic (Africa, linked to Epstein-Barr and malaria), sporadic (abdomen), immunodeficiency-associated (HIV)
Emergency Risk of tumour lysis syndrome when treatment starts
Treatment Short intensive chemotherapy with rituximab
Cure > 90% in children; ~80% in adults

History

Surgeon Denis Burkitt described jaw tumours in Ugandan children in 1958. In 1964, Anthony Epstein and Yvonne Barr found in these cells the first virus known to cause human cancer, Epstein-Barr virus.

Treatment

Hospital admission with intensive fluids and drugs (rasburicase) to prevent tumour lysis; short intensive chemotherapy (CODOX-M/IVAC, DA-EPOCH-R or paediatric protocols such as LMB), with rituximab and central nervous system prophylaxis. In Africa, lack of access to these treatments still costs many lives.

See also: Lymphomas, Childhood cancer, Nasopharyngeal cancer.
