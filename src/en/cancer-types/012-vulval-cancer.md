## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | About 47,000 cases a year worldwide; around 4% of gynaecological cancers |
| **Who** | Mainly women over 65; increasingly younger women because of HPV |
| **Signs** | Persistent itch, lump, ulcer or patch that does not heal |
| **Main type** | Squamous cell carcinoma (90%) |
| **Treatment** | Surgery with sentinel node; radiotherapy and chemoradiotherapy |
| **Survival** | Over 85% if lymph nodes are not involved |

## What it is and why it happens

The **vulva** includes the outer and inner lips, the clitoris and the vaginal opening. 90% of its cancers are **squamous cell carcinomas**, which develop along two paths:

- **HPV-associated**: in younger women and smokers, from a precursor lesion (high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion, formerly usual VIN).
- **HPV-independent**: in older women, on skin with long-standing **lichen sclerosus**, through a lesion called differentiated VIN. It often carries *TP53* mutations and has a worse outlook.

Rare types include **vulval melanoma**, basal cell carcinoma, **extramammary Paget disease** and Bartholin gland adenocarcinoma.

## Risk factors and prevention

- **HPV** infection; the vaccine prevents most cases arising this way.
- Untreated **lichen sclerosus**: treating it with potent steroids and yearly checks lowers risk.
- Smoking, weakened immunity (HIV, transplants), age.

## Symptoms

Itching lasting weeks, burning, pain, a white, red or dark area, a lump or an ulcer that does not heal. Many women seek help late out of embarrassment, or are treated for months as if it were an infection. **A lesion that does not improve with treatment should be biopsied**.

## Diagnosis

Magnified examination (vulvoscopy) and **biopsy**. Groin lymph nodes are assessed by examination, ultrasound and, if needed, CT, MRI or PET-CT.

## Treatment

- **Surgery**: wide local excision with clear margins, less disfiguring than the old radical vulvectomy. **Sentinel node** biopsy for tumours under 4 cm without palpable nodes, avoiding full groin node dissection and its swelling (GROINSS-V study).
- **Radiotherapy** to the groins if nodes are involved, or **chemoradiotherapy** with cisplatin for locally advanced tumours to shrink them or instead of very extensive surgery.
- **Advanced or recurrent disease**: chemotherapy; immunotherapy (pembrolizumab) for PD-L1-positive or microsatellite-unstable tumours.
- Precursor lesions are treated with surgery, laser or **imiquimod** cream.

## Outlook

It depends mainly on the **lymph nodes**: over 85% at five years without node involvement, falling sharply with several nodes or spread through the node capsule.

## Afterwards

Lifelong check-ups, with self-examination, because new tumours can appear in the remaining vulva. Physiotherapy and compression stockings for leg **lymphoedema**; support for sexuality and body image.
