## During treatment

The main goal is to **maintain weight and muscle**. Unintended weight loss makes treatment harder to tolerate. Some tips:

- **Small, frequent meals**, making the most of times when appetite is better.
- Enough **protein**: eggs, dairy, fish, meat, pulses.
- Good **hydration**.
- If your immunity is low, follow the **food safety** advice you are given (avoid risky raw foods, wash well, cook thoroughly).
- If you are losing weight or cannot eat, ask for a **dietitian**: there are supplements and strategies for every problem.

## Beware of myths

- No diet **cures** cancer. Very restrictive diets (alkaline, prolonged fasting, cutting out food groups) can cause malnutrition.
- **Sugar does not specifically feed** the tumour: all cells use glucose. A balanced diet is the sensible approach.
- **Supplements and herbal products** can interfere with chemotherapy, radiotherapy or targeted therapy (for example St John's wort or grapefruit). Always ask before taking them.

## Exercise is a treatment too

The evidence on **physical activity** in people with cancer is strong:

- It reduces cancer-related **fatigue**, anxiety and depression.
- It improves fitness, strength and quality of life.
- It helps people tolerate treatment better.
- In **colon cancer**, a trial published in 2025 showed that a structured exercise programme after adjuvant chemotherapy reduced relapses and improved survival.

Guidelines recommend, if possible, about **150 minutes a week** of moderate aerobic activity (brisk walking, cycling) and **strength exercises** twice a week, adapted to each person. Something is better than nothing: start gradually. Check first if you have bone metastases, very low immunity, severe anaemia or heart problems.

## After treatment

Keeping a healthy weight, eating plenty of vegetables, pulses and whole grains, limiting processed meat and alcohol, and moving every day are the recommendations for survivors too.
