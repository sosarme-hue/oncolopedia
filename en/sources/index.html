Sources

Sources and library

Where to read more, cross-check and verify: reliable oncology sources for patients, families and professionals, with what to know about each.

A reliable source does not replace your care team. If a result or a news story worries you, talk to your oncologist or your doctor.

Information for patients and families

National Cancer Institute (NCI) ↗
Extensive, reviewed information, including PDQ summaries for patients. Cancer Information Service: 1-800-4-CANCER.
Cancer Research UK ↗
Clear information on every cancer type, causes, screening and statistics.
Macmillan Cancer Support ↗
Practical, emotional and financial support. Support line: 0808 808 00 00.
American Cancer Society ↗
Information on cancer types, treatment and survivorship, and the annual Cancer Facts & Figures.
Childhood Cancer International ↗
Global network of parent and survivor organisations in childhood cancer.
World Marrow Donor Association ↗
Links stem cell donor registries worldwide and explains how to become a donor.

Clinical reference

NCI · PDQ ↗
Evidence summaries for professionals and patients, updated regularly. Check the update date.
ESMO · Patient guides ↗
European Society for Medical Oncology. Patient versions of its clinical guidelines.
WHO Classification of Tumours ↗
The international reference classification, with histological and molecular criteria. For professionals.
SEER · ICD-O-3 ↗
Tumour coding materials. Not a substitute for a pathology report.

Cancer statistics

IARC · Global Cancer Observatory ↗
Incidence and mortality estimates by country, sex, age and site (GLOBOCAN).
WHO · Cancer ↗
World Health Organization fact sheet with the main figures and risk factors.
Cancer Research UK · Cancer statistics ↗
Incidence, mortality and survival in the UK by cancer type.
SEER · Cancer Stat Facts ↗
Incidence, mortality and survival in the United States by cancer type.

Prevention

European Code Against Cancer ↗
IARC and European Commission recommendations to reduce cancer risk.
IARC · Monographs ↗
Assessment of which agents cause cancer. It grades the strength of the evidence, not the size of the risk.
US Preventive Services Task Force ↗
Evidence-based recommendations on cancer screening in the United States.

Trials and medicines

ClinicalTrials.gov ↗
Worldwide register of clinical trials. A registered trial does not mean the treatment works.
EU Clinical Trials (CTIS) ↗
European Union clinical trials register, searchable by country.
European Medicines Agency ↗
Information on medicines authorised in the European Union.
Farmacos.ai ↗
The medicines encyclopedia of our network, with profiles of active ingredients.

Reference library

Textbooks and books we rely on to write and review the content.

  • Mukherjee, S. The Emperor of All Maladies: A Biography of Cancer. Scribner, 2010.

Oncolopedia

An encyclopedia of oncology for the general public: science, health and history. It does not provide diagnosis, treatment or individual second opinions.

Have a symptom that will not go away, or worried about a diagnosis? Talk to your doctor or your cancer team. In the UK, Macmillan Support Line: 0808 808 00 00; in the US, the NCI Cancer Information Service: 1-800-4-CANCER.
