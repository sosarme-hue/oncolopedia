## A doctor with a microscope

Rudolf Virchow studied medicine in Berlin and worked at the Charité hospital. He was one of the first to use the **microscope** systematically to study disease. In 1845 he described a condition in which the blood had an excess of white cells and named it ***Leukämie***, "white blood": **leukaemia**.

## Cellular pathology

In 1858 he published *Die Cellularpathologie*, a book that changed medicine. Its central idea is summed up in the Latin phrase ***omnis cellula e cellula***: every cell comes from another cell. Diseases, he argued, are not imbalances of humours but changes in cells.

Applied to cancer, this meant that a tumour does not arise from a fluid or from nothing, but from **the body's own cells dividing abnormally**. It is the basis of today's pathology: every cancer diagnosis is confirmed by looking at cells under the microscope.

## Right and wrong

Virchow described **thrombosis** and embolism, and the left supraclavicular lymph node that enlarges in some digestive cancers bears his name (**Virchow's node**). He thought cancer arose from chronic irritation of tissues, an idea partly correct (chronic inflammation and cancer are linked), though he was wrong to deny that tumour cells travel in the blood to form metastases.

## Doctor and politician

He was also a member of parliament and a champion of public health. After studying a typhus epidemic in Silesia in 1848 he concluded that poverty and lack of education caused disease, and wrote that "medicine is a social science".
