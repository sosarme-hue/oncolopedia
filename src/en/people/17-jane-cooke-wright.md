## The daughter of a pioneer

Jane Cooke Wright was the daughter of Louis T. Wright, one of the first African American surgeons on the staff of New York hospitals, who founded the Cancer Research Center at Harlem Hospital in 1948. Jane qualified in medicine in 1945 and in 1949 joined her father to test the new anticancer drugs, at a time when chemotherapy was a fringe and risky practice.

## Chemotherapy as a science

Wright and her team studied hundreds of patients with rigorous methods. One of her most advanced ideas was to **grow cells from each patient's tumour in the laboratory** and test several drugs on them to choose the most effective, a forerunner of personalised medicine. They described responses to **methotrexate** in solid tumours such as breast cancer and mycosis fungoides.

## Opening doors

After her father's death she ran the centre at 33. In 1964 she was one of the seven founders of the **American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO)**, the only woman in the group. She was also associate dean of New York Medical College, the highest post an African American woman had held at a US medical school. She developed catheter techniques to deliver chemotherapy directly into hard-to-reach tumours.
