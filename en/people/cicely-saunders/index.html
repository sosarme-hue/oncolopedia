Cicely Saunders

The British nurse, social worker and doctor who founded the modern palliative care movement and the concept of "total pain".

Three professions

Cicely Saunders was first a nurse, during the Second World War, until a back injury took her out of the profession. She then trained as a medical social worker. Seeing how dying patients were treated in hospitals, she realised she needed a doctor's authority to change things and, at 33, studied medicine.

Total pain

She researched the regular use of morphine, given on a schedule to prevent pain rather than waiting for it to appear. And she proposed the concept of "total pain": the suffering of a person with advanced illness has a physical dimension, but also psychological, social and spiritual ones, and all must be addressed.

St Christopher's

In 1967 she founded St Christopher's Hospice in London, the first modern hospice combining clinical care, teaching and research. Its model spread around the world and gave rise to palliative care, which is now part of the care of any serious illness, from diagnosis.

One of her sayings sums up her approach: "You matter because you are you, and you matter to the end of your life."
