## Before you start

Ask your team for **a phone number to call, day and night**, and for written instructions. Many hospitals have an oncology nurse helpline or a day unit you can call directly. Keep the number on your phone and on the fridge, together with the name of your treatment and the date of your last dose.

This page is a general guide. **Your team's instructions always come first.**

## Call or go to the emergency department straight away

- **Fever of 38 °C or more**, or chills, especially in the 7-14 days after chemotherapy. It may be **febrile neutropenia**, an infection with low white cells. It is an emergency even if you feel well. Do not take paracetamol to bring it down before calling.
- **Difficulty breathing**, chest pain or a sudden racing heartbeat.
- **Bleeding** that does not stop, vomiting blood, black stools.
- **Confusion**, drowsiness, a seizure, sudden weakness in an arm or leg, or difficulty speaking.
- **Swelling and pain in one leg** (possible clot).
- **Vomiting** that prevents you from drinking for more than 24 hours, or very little urine.
- **Severe diarrhoea** (more than 6-7 times a day) or with blood.
- **Back pain with weakness or numbness in the legs**, or loss of bladder or bowel control, if you have a known spinal metastasis.

In an emergency, call **112** in Europe, **999** in the UK or **911** in the US.

## Report the same day

- Diarrhoea or vomiting that is moderate but persistent.
- Mouth sores that stop you eating.
- A new skin rash, especially if it is extensive or blistering.
- Burning when passing urine.
- Pain, redness or swelling around a port or PICC line.
- New or worsening pain that your usual medicine does not control.

## Can wait for your next appointment

- Usual tiredness, if it is not getting much worse.
- Mild hair loss, changes in taste or nails.
- Mild constipation that improves with diet or laxatives.
- Mild tingling in the fingers. Tell your team at the next visit: it may mean adjusting the dose.

See also: [Side effects](../side-effects/), [Pain and other symptoms](../pain-and-other-symptoms/).

## Immunotherapy is different

Immunotherapy can cause **inflammation of almost any organ**, sometimes months after starting or even after stopping it. Call your team about new **diarrhoea**, a **cough or breathlessness**, **intense tiredness**, a **rash**, **yellow skin**, headache or changes in vision. Caught early, these effects are well controlled. Always say you are on immunotherapy, including in the emergency department. See [Immunotherapy](../../treatments/immunotherapy/).
