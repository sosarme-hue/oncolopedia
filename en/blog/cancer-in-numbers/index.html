Cancer in numbers: what the latest global data say

About 20 million new cases and 9.7 million deaths a year. Which cancers are most common, what has changed and what can be prevented.

The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) publishes GLOBOCAN estimates every few years, the most complete picture of cancer worldwide. The latest, for 2022, were published in 2024.

The big numbers

  • About 20 million new cancer cases a year (not counting non-melanoma skin cancers).
  • About 9.7 million deaths.
  • Around one in five people will develop cancer during their lifetime.

The most common

Cancer Share of new cases
Lung 12.4%
Female breast 11.6%
Colorectal 9.6%
Prostate 7.3%
Stomach 4.9%

Lung cancer also causes the most deaths, almost one in five.

Big differences between countries

More cancers are diagnosed in high-income countries, partly because of ageing and screening, but relative mortality is much higher in countries with fewer resources, where diagnosis comes late and treatments are lacking. Cervical cancer, almost eliminable with vaccination and screening, is still one of the leading causes of cancer death in women in many African countries.

Towards 2050

IARC estimates that cases could exceed 35 million a year by 2050, mainly because of ageing and population growth. But 30 to 50% of cancers could be prevented by avoiding tobacco, alcohol, obesity and some infections, and through screening programmes.

See also: Why is there more cancer than before?, The European Code Against Cancer.
