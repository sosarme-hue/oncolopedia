## What it measures

**Five-year survival** is the percentage of people in a group still alive five years after diagnosis. If it is 80% for a cancer, it means that of every hundred people diagnosed in a given period, about eighty were still alive five years later.

Registries often use **net survival**, which removes deaths from other causes, so countries and periods can be compared.

## What it does not mean

- **It is not a deadline**: it does not mean people live only five years. Many live for decades, and for many cancers reaching five years without disease is practically a cure.
- **It does not predict what will happen to one person**: it is an average of a group with very different ages, stages, subtypes and circumstances.
- **It looks backwards**: today's figures describe people diagnosed years ago, with older treatments. Current survival is usually better.

## Median survival

Trials often use the **median**: the time at which half the group is still alive. A median of two years does not mean that is each person's time: half live longer, some much longer. The biologist Stephen Jay Gould explained this in a famous essay, "The median isn't the message", written after his diagnosis of mesothelioma, with which he lived another twenty years.

## Ask about your own case

If you want to know your outlook, ask your team: they can explain it for your cancer type, stage and situation. It is also fine not to want the figures.
