Joan Massagué

The Barcelona-born scientist at Memorial Sloan Kettering who has discovered how cancer cells spread and survive in other organs: the biology of metastasis.

From Barcelona to New York

Joan Massagué studied pharmacy at the University of Barcelona and moved to the United States in 1979. Since 1989 he has worked at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center in New York, where he led the Cancer Biology and Genetics programme and, since 2014, the Sloan Kettering Institute.

The signals that stop division

For two decades he worked out how TGF-beta works: a signal that stops healthy cells dividing and that tumours learn to ignore or turn to their advantage. He described its receptors and the chain of SMAD proteins that carries the signal to the nucleus.

Metastasis

Nine out of ten cancer deaths are due to metastases. Massagué's laboratory has identified the genes that let breast cancer cells colonise bone, lung or brain, and has described how metastatic cells can remain dormant for years, hidden from the immune system, before waking. Understanding these stages opens the door to treatments that prevent relapse.

See also: Bone metastases, Brain metastases, Mariano Barbacid.
