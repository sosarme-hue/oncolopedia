## What it is

According to the WHO, **palliative care** improves the quality of life of people with serious illness and their families by preventing and relieving physical, psychological, social and spiritual suffering. It is provided by a team of doctors, nurses, psychologists and social workers, in hospital, in the community or at home.

## Not only for the end

A common mistake is to think palliative care means "there is nothing more to be done". In fact:

- It can start **at diagnosis** of advanced cancer, **alongside** chemotherapy, immunotherapy or other treatments.
- A 2010 trial in advanced lung cancer showed that patients receiving early palliative care had **better quality of life and less depression**, and also lived somewhat longer.
- Oncology society guidelines recommend bringing it in early in advanced cancer.

## What it covers

- **Pain**: can almost always be well controlled with medicines, including correctly used opioids, and other techniques. Properly prescribed morphine does not shorten life or cause the addiction problems people fear in this setting.
- **Other symptoms**: breathlessness, nausea, constipation, tiredness, loss of appetite, insomnia, anxiety.
- **Information and decisions**: understanding the situation, planning care and stating one's own wishes (**advance care planning** and **advance directives**).
- **Support for family** and carers, including in bereavement.

## At the end of life

When the illness progresses, the focus is on **comfort**: relieving symptoms, avoiding tests and treatments that bring no benefit, and caring for the person where they prefer, often at home. **Palliative sedation** relieves symptoms that do not respond to other treatments and is a medical practice distinct from euthanasia or assisted dying, which is regulated very differently from country to country.

## How to ask for it

Ask your oncologist or family doctor. Most countries have hospital palliative care teams, hospices and community services, although access varies. Charities such as Marie Curie and Macmillan in the UK also offer support.
