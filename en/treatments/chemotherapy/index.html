Chemotherapy

Cytotoxic drugs destroy fast-dividing cells. What they are used for, how they are given and how their side effects are prevented and treated.

What it is

Chemotherapy is treatment with cytotoxic drugs, which damage cells that divide quickly, such as cancer cells. Because they also affect healthy fast-dividing cells (in the bone marrow, hair or gut), they cause characteristic side effects. It has been used since the 1940s, when nitrogen mustard was tested in lymphomas and aminopterin in childhood leukaemia.

What it is used for

  • To cure some cancers on its own, such as testicular cancer and many lymphomas and leukaemias.
  • Neoadjuvant: before surgery, to shrink the tumour and test its sensitivity.
  • Adjuvant: after surgery, to destroy cells left behind and reduce the risk of relapse.
  • With radiotherapy (chemoradiotherapy), to boost its effect.
  • Advanced disease: to control cancer, extend life and relieve symptoms.

How it is given

Almost always intravenously, in a day unit, sometimes through a line or port under the skin. Some are taken as tablets. It is given in cycles, with treatment days and rest days to let the body recover. Several drugs are combined to increase effectiveness, often with other treatments such as immunotherapy or antibodies.

Side effects

They depend on the drug, the dose and the person. Not everyone gets all of them:

  • Low immunity (neutropenia), with a risk of infection. A fever during chemotherapy is an emergency: follow your team's instructions and seek help without delay.
  • Tiredness, anaemia, nausea and vomiting (now very well prevented with anti-sickness drugs), mouth sores, diarrhoea or constipation.
  • Hair loss with some drugs; scalp cooling can reduce it in some cases.
  • Tingling in the hands and feet (neuropathy), taste changes, effects on fertility.

Most are temporary. Tell your team about them: many can be prevented or treated by adjusting the dose or with medication.

Worth asking

What is the aim of treatment? How many cycles? What effects should I expect and which should I report? Can I keep working? Should I preserve my fertility? Can I take other medicines or herbal products? Some supplements interfere with chemotherapy.
