## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | About 30,000 cases a year worldwide; the UK has one of the highest rates (around 2,700 a year) |
| **Cause** | **Asbestos** in 80%; latency of 20 to 50 years |
| **Site** | Pleura (80-90%), peritoneum, pericardium, tunica vaginalis |
| **Types** | Epithelioid (better outlook), sarcomatoid, biphasic |
| **Treatment** | Dual immunotherapy or chemotherapy; surgery in very selected cases |

## What it is

The **mesothelium** is the thin membrane lining the lungs (pleura), abdomen (peritoneum) and heart (pericardium). Mesothelioma arises in it and grows as a sheet wrapping around the lung.

## Asbestos

**Asbestos** fibres were used in asbestos cement, insulation, brakes, shipyards and construction. Once inhaled they stay in the pleura for decades. Most European countries banned it by 2005 (the UK in 1999), but cases keep appearing because of the long latency, and it remains in many buildings. At risk are exposed workers, their families (through work clothes) and people living near factories. Other factors: previous radiotherapy and inherited predisposition from ***BAP1*** mutations.

It is a recognised **occupational disease**: affected people and their families are entitled to benefits and compensation, with support from asbestos victims' groups.

## Symptoms

Breathlessness from **pleural effusion**, persistent chest pain, cough, weight loss, sweats. Peritoneal disease causes swelling and abdominal pain.

## Diagnosis

CT, PET-CT and drainage of pleural fluid; **pleural biopsy by thoracoscopy** is usually needed, as fluid alone is not enough. Immunohistochemistry (calretinin, loss of BAP1) distinguishes mesothelioma from metastases of other cancers.

## Treatment

- **Immunotherapy with nivolumab and ipilimumab** (CheckMate 743 trial), which improves survival, especially in non-epithelioid types.
- **Chemotherapy** with cisplatin or carboplatin and pemetrexed, to which pembrolizumab or bevacizumab may be added.
- **Surgery**: pleurectomy/decortication is reserved for very selected cases in expert centres; extrapleural pneumonectomy has been almost abandoned after the MARS trial.
- **Fluid control**: an indwelling pleural catheter or talc pleurodesis.
- **Peritoneal mesothelioma**: cytoreductive surgery with heated intraperitoneal chemotherapy (**HIPEC**) in specialist centres, with good survival in selected cases.
- Early palliative care for pain and breathlessness.

## Outlook

Median survival for pleural mesothelioma is 1.5 to 2 years with current treatments, longer for epithelioid. Peritoneal disease treated with surgery and HIPEC can exceed 50% at five years.
