## Genetic is not the same as inherited

Cancer is always a **genetic** disease, because it arises from changes in cells' DNA. But almost all those changes are **acquired** during life in a particular cell, and are not passed on to children.

Only **5 to 10%** of cancers are due to an **inherited variant** that greatly increases risk, such as those in *BRCA1* and *BRCA2* or in Lynch syndrome.

## Having a relative with cancer

Cancer is common: about one in two or three people will have it during their lifetime. So it is normal for almost every family to have cases, without that meaning an inherited predisposition. Sometimes relatives share habits or exposures, such as smoking.

## When to suspect a predisposition

- Several relatives on the same side with the same or related cancers (breast and ovary; bowel and endometrium).
- Cancers at a young age, for example before 50.
- One person with two different cancers.
- Unusual cancers, such as breast cancer in a man.

In these cases, talk to your family doctor, who can refer you to a **cancer genetics service**.
