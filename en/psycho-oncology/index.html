Psycho-oncology

Caring for the mind too

The emotional impact of cancer on the person and their family: fear, anxiety and depression, fear of recurrence, talking to children, body image, grief and where to get psychological help.

01

What is psycho-oncology

The specialty that cares for the emotional impact of cancer on patients and their families, and studies how psychological and social factors influence the illness. Who provides it and when to ask for it.

The emotional impact of the diagnosis

What people usually feel when they are diagnosed with cancer, why reactions change over time, what is normal and which signs suggest asking for specialist help.

Anxiety, depression and fear of recurrence

How to recognise anxiety and depression during and after cancer, what fear of recurrence is, and which psychological and drug treatments have been shown to help.

Read →04

Talking to children about cancer

Children notice when something is happening at home. How to explain a parent's diagnosis according to their age, what questions they usually ask and which signs show they need help.

Read →05

Body image, self-esteem and relationships

Scars, hair loss, a mastectomy or an ostomy change the way people see themselves. How these changes affect self-esteem and relationships, and what helps recovery.

Read →06

End of life, grief and family

When cancer advances, care focuses on comfort, dignity and the person's preferences. How to prepare, what rights exist and how to support the family's grief.

Read →07

Where to get psychological help

Resources for people with cancer and their families: the hospital's psycho-oncology unit, primary care, charities offering free support and crisis lines, in Spain, the UK and the US.
