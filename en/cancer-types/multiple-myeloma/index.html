Cancer types

Multiple myeloma

A cancer of bone marrow plasma cells that affects the bones, kidneys and blood. It is not usually curable yet, but new treatments can control it for many years.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
What it is Cancer of plasma cells, which make antibodies
Who Mostly people over 65
Signs (CRAB) high Calcium, Renal failure, Anaemia and Bone lesions
Precursor Monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS), common and almost always harmless
Outlook Survival has improved greatly; many patients now live more than ten years

What it is

Plasma cells are mature B lymphocytes that make antibodies. In myeloma, one of them multiplies out of control in the bone marrow and produces large amounts of a single antibody, the monoclonal protein or M protein, which can be measured in blood and urine.

Almost all myelomas are preceded by a silent phase called monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS), present in about 3% of people over 50. Only about 1% a year progress to myeloma, so MGUS is monitored, not treated.

Symptoms

  • Bone pain, especially in the back, and fractures after minor knocks.
  • Tiredness from anaemia.
  • Repeated infections.
  • Thirst, constipation or confusion from high calcium, and kidney problems.

Diagnosis

Blood and urine tests with electrophoresis and immunofixation to detect the monoclonal protein, serum free light chains, bone marrow biopsy and bone imaging (low-dose whole-body CT or PET-CT). Genetic tests on the myeloma cells define risk.

Treatment

Only myeloma that causes organ damage or has high-risk features is treated:

  • Combinations of immunomodulatory drugs (lenalidomide, pomalidomide), proteasome inhibitors (bortezomib, carfilzomib), monoclonal antibodies (daratumumab, isatuximab) and steroids.
  • Autologous stem cell transplant in patients fit enough, followed by maintenance.
  • On relapse, CAR-T and bispecific antibodies targeting BCMA or other targets.
  • Supportive care: bone-protecting drugs, radiotherapy for pain, infection prevention and kidney care.

See also: Stem cell transplant and CAR-T, Leukaemias.
