## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | Rising: about 5-7 cases per 100,000 people a year |
| **Where** | Small bowel (ileum), rectum, appendix, stomach, pancreas, colon |
| **Grades** | G1, G2, G3 by Ki-67 index; neuroendocrine carcinomas are very aggressive |
| **Typical symptom** | Carcinoid syndrome: facial flushing, diarrhoea, sometimes heart valve damage |
| **Treatment** | Surgery; somatostatin analogues, lutetium-177, everolimus, chemotherapy |

## What they are

**Neuroendocrine cells** are scattered throughout the gut and make hormones such as **serotonin**. Their tumours (NETs) are very varied:

- **Well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumours** (G1, G2 and G3): slow or intermediate growth.
- Poorly differentiated **neuroendocrine carcinomas** (small or large cell): aggressive, treated like small cell lung cancer.

Pancreatic ones have [their own sheet](../pancreatic-neuroendocrine-tumours/). **Appendix** NETs are usually found after appendicitis and are almost always cured by appendicectomy.

## Symptoms

Many cause no symptoms and are found by chance. **Ileal** tumours can cause colicky pain and obstruction, sometimes from intense scarring of the mesentery. When there are **liver metastases**, serotonin reaches the circulation and causes **carcinoid syndrome**: sudden facial flushing, diarrhoea, wheezing and, over years, damage to the right heart valves (carcinoid heart disease). Mature cheese, alcohol or stress can trigger attacks.

## Diagnosis

- **Gallium-68 DOTATATE PET-CT** (or copper-64), which detects somatostatin receptors.
- CT and liver MRI, endoscopy and endoscopic ultrasound.
- **Ki-67** on biopsy for grading.
- Tests: **chromogranin A** and 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (5-HIAA) in 24-hour urine or blood.
- Echocardiogram if carcinoid syndrome is present.
- Consider inherited syndromes (MEN1, von Hippel-Lindau, neurofibromatosis type 1).

## Treatment

- **Surgery** to the primary tumour and mesenteric nodes; also liver metastases where possible.
- **Somatostatin analogues** (octreotide, lanreotide) by monthly injection: control symptoms and slow growth (PROMID and CLARINET trials).
- **Peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT)**: **lutetium-177 DOTATATE**, a "Trojan horse" carrying radiation to cells with receptors. It clearly improved control in small bowel NETs (NETTER-1 trial) and first line in G2-G3 (NETTER-2).
- **Everolimus**, tyrosine kinase inhibitors (cabozantinib) and chemotherapy (capecitabine with temozolomide).
- **Liver-directed treatment**: embolisation, radioembolisation, ablation.
- **Telotristat** for resistant carcinoid diarrhoea.
- Neuroendocrine carcinomas: platinum and etoposide.

## Outlook

Well-differentiated tumours often progress slowly: many people live more than 10 years even with metastases. Neuroendocrine carcinomas are serious. Treatment in **ENETS centres of excellence** with multidisciplinary teams is advisable.
