## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 1 in 15,000-20,000 live births; about 8,000 cases a year worldwide |
| **Who** | Children under 5, almost always under 2 |
| **Genetics** | *RB1* gene: 40% inherited (both eyes, at younger ages) |
| **Sign** | **Leukocoria** (white pupil reflex) and squint |
| **Cure** | Over 95% in Europe; under 30% in many low-income countries |

## What it is

A tumour of the developing **retina**. Its study led to Alfred Knudson's **"two-hit" hypothesis** (1971): two mutations of the *RB1* gene are needed for it to develop. In inherited forms the child is born with one already present, so tumours are bilateral and early. *RB1* was the first **tumour suppressor gene** identified (1986).

## Signs

- **Leukocoria**: a white pupil, often seen in a **flash photo** instead of the usual red eye.
- **Squint** (a turned eye).
- A red, painful eye or poor vision.

With a white pupil, see the GP or an eye specialist **without delay**.

## Treatment

In specialist units (in the UK, Birmingham Children's Hospital and the Royal London Hospital):
- **Intra-arterial chemotherapy** (directly into the eye's artery) or intravitreal.
- Systemic chemotherapy, laser, cryotherapy, brachytherapy.
- **Enucleation** if the tumour is very large or the eye has no useful vision.
- Genetic testing of the child and family; eye checks for siblings.

## Afterwards

*RB1* mutation carriers have a higher risk of **second cancers** (especially osteosarcoma and other sarcomas, and melanoma), so they should avoid unnecessary radiation and have long-term follow-up.
