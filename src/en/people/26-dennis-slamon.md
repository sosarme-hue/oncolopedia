## An amplified gene

In the 1980s Dennis Slamon, an oncologist at the University of California, Los Angeles, was analysing breast cancer samples for oncogene changes. In 1987 he reported that about 25-30% of tumours had many copies of the ***HER2*** gene (also called *neu* or *ERBB2*), and that those patients had a worse outlook.

## An antibody against HER2

The company Genentech had created an antibody that blocked the HER2 protein. With little funding and scepticism within the company itself, Slamon led the clinical trials with support from donors. In 1998 **trastuzumab** (Herceptin) was approved for HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer, and in 2005 it was shown that, added after surgery, it **halved recurrences**.

## Precision medicine

It was one of the first examples of choosing a treatment according to a change in the tumour, measured with a test. HER2-positive breast cancer went from one of the worst outlooks to very good results. His story was told in the film *Living Proof* (2008). Slamon also took part in developing CDK4/6 inhibitors.
