## The immune system's brakes

T lymphocytes carry molecules that act as **brakes** to stop them attacking healthy tissue. Two researchers, working separately, studied two of them.

## James Allison and CTLA-4

James Allison, at the University of California, Berkeley, studied the protein **CTLA-4**. He reasoned that blocking it might let lymphocytes attack tumours. In 1996 he showed that an antibody against CTLA-4 made tumours disappear in mice. It took years to convince industry. The resulting drug, **ipilimumab**, was approved in 2011 for advanced melanoma, and some treated patients are still alive more than ten years later.

## Tasuku Honjo and PD-1

Tasuku Honjo, at Kyoto University, discovered the protein **PD-1** in 1992 in dying lymphocytes, without knowing its function. His group later showed it was another brake. Antibodies against PD-1, such as **nivolumab** and **pembrolizumab**, approved in 2014, proved effective in many more cancer types and with fewer side effects than anti-CTLA-4.

## A new way to treat cancer

In 2018 they shared the **Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine**. Their discoveries created a fourth pillar of cancer treatment, alongside surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy. Checkpoint inhibitors are now used in dozens of indications, although only some patients respond, and research is looking at how to widen that group.
