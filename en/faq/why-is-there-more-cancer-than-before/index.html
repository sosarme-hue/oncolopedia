FAQ

Why is there more cancer than before?

The number of cases is rising, but the risk of dying from cancer at each age is falling. The reasons are ageing, earlier diagnosis and some habits.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

More cases, lower death rates by age

It is true that more cancers are diagnosed each year: worldwide, cases rose from about 14 million in 2012 to about 20 million in 2022. But that does not mean cancer is "a modern epidemic". There are several explanations:

  • Ageing: cancer is much more common with age, and we are living longer. This is the main reason.
  • Population growth.
  • Better diagnosis: screening and imaging find more tumours, sometimes ones that would never have caused problems.
  • Changing habits: smoking among women from the 1970s, obesity, inactivity and alcohol.

When people of the same age are compared, cancer death rates have been falling in Europe and North America since the 1990s, especially among men, thanks to less smoking, earlier diagnosis and better treatment.

A real concern: cancer in young adults

In several countries the incidence of some cancers is rising in people under 50, especially colorectal cancer. Possible causes such as obesity, diet or the gut microbiome are being studied. The absolute risk in young people is still low, but symptoms should not be ignored because of age.

See also: The European Code Against Cancer, Symptoms not to ignore.
