The photograph that opens Oncolopedia is a portrait of **Marie Curie** taken around 1921, the year she travelled to the United States to receive a gram of radium bought with a collection by American women, for her laboratory in Paris. It is in the public domain and comes from Wikimedia Commons.

## Why her

We chose her for three reasons:

- **The science**: the discovery of radium in 1898 opened the way to radiotherapy, which about half of people with cancer receive today.
- **Perseverance**: she was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize and the only person honoured in two different sciences, at a time when universities were closed to women.
- **The warning**: Curie died from exposure to the radiation she studied. The history of oncology is made of advances, but also of mistakes and a price paid. Telling it in full helps explain why today we demand trials and protection.

## From radium to modern radiotherapy

For decades radium was used in needles and applicators placed next to tumours. This was **Curietherapy**, the forerunner of today's brachytherapy, which now uses safer sources and is planned with three-dimensional imaging. External beam radiotherapy, with linear accelerators, now treats a tumour with millimetre precision.
