Biomarkers and molecular profiling

What biomarkers are, how a tumour's genetic changes are studied and why finding a target does not guarantee an effective treatment.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

What a biomarker is

A biomarker is a measurable feature that tells us something about the tumour. In oncology they are used to:

  • Classify the tumour precisely (for example, subtypes of breast cancer or glioma).
  • Estimate the outlook.
  • Predict whether a treatment will work: the most useful in practice.

Examples that change treatment

Biomarker Tumours What it indicates
Hormone receptors Breast Benefit from hormone therapy
HER2 Breast, stomach Benefit from anti-HER2 drugs
EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, KRAS G12C, MET, RET, NTRK Lung and others Targeted drugs in tablet form
KRAS, NRAS, BRAF Colorectal Whether anti-EGFR drugs will work
BRCA1/2 and homologous recombination Ovary, breast, prostate, pancreas PARP inhibitors
Microsatellite instability Colorectal, endometrium and others Very good response to immunotherapy
PD-L1 Lung and others Helps decide on immunotherapy
BCR-ABL Chronic myeloid leukaemia Tyrosine kinase inhibitors

How they are studied

With immunohistochemistry, in situ hybridisation (FISH) and next-generation sequencing (NGS), which analyses tens or hundreds of genes at once in a tumour sample or, in some cases, in blood (liquid biopsy).

Somatic is not the same as inherited

Changes found in the tumour are usually somatic: they arose in the cancer cells and are not inherited. Sometimes, however, tumour testing reveals a possible germline variant, present in every cell and transmissible to children. In that case genetic counselling and a blood test are offered.

What to know

  • Finding a target does not guarantee a response or access to the drug. Tumour type, the level of evidence and the drug's approval all matter.
  • Many panels find changes with no available treatment, or of uncertain significance. A molecular tumour board helps interpret them.
  • Some treatments are now approved for the change whatever the organ of the tumour ("tumour-agnostic" treatments), such as NTRK inhibitors or immunotherapy for tumours with microsatellite instability.
