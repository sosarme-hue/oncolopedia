## Sea urchins and chromosomes

Theodor Boveri worked in Würzburg with **sea urchin** eggs. He saw that when an egg was fertilised by two sperm, the resulting cells shared out their chromosomes unevenly when dividing and the embryo developed abnormally. He concluded that each chromosome carries different information and that the full set is needed for normal development.

## A hypothesis about cancer

In 1914 he published a short book, *Zur Frage der Entstehung maligner Tumoren* ("Concerning the origin of malignant tumours"). He proposed that cancer begins in **a single cell** with an abnormal combination of chromosomes, which passes the abnormality on to its descendants. He imagined chromosomes that stimulate division and others that restrain it, and that cancer appears when the brakes are lost or the stimuli multiply.

## Confirmed decades later

With no tools to test it, his idea was forgotten. In 1960 the **Philadelphia chromosome** was discovered in chronic myeloid leukaemia, and in the 1970s and 1980s **oncogenes** (accelerators) and **tumour suppressor genes** (brakes). Boveri had, unknowingly, described the genetic basis of cancer.
