## What screening is

**Screening** offers a test to people **without symptoms** to find a cancer at an early stage, or a precancerous change, when treatment works best. To be worthwhile, it must be shown to **reduce deaths** and its benefits must outweigh its harms.

## Typical programmes

Ages and intervals vary by country. A broad picture, based on the 2022 European Council recommendation and the guidance of bodies such as the UK National Screening Committee and the US Preventive Services Task Force:

| Cancer | Who | Test | How often |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Breast** | Women around 50 to 69-74 (some countries start at 40 or 45) | Mammogram | Every 2 or 3 years |
| **Bowel** | Men and women around 50 to 74 (from 45 in the US) | Faecal immunochemical test (FIT) and colonoscopy if positive; colonoscopy in some countries | Every 2 years (FIT) |
| **Cervical** | Women and people with a cervix, around 25 to 64-65 | HPV test, with cytology if positive | Every 5 years (HPV) |
| **Lung** | Current or former heavy smokers, around 50 to 80 | Low-dose CT | Yearly or every 2 years |

Programmes usually **send invitations**. If you are in the age group and have not been invited, ask your doctor. The European Union also recommended in 2022 exploring **prostate** screening (PSA with MRI) and **stomach** screening in high-incidence areas.

## Benefits and harms

- **Benefits**: fewer deaths from these cancers and less aggressive treatment thanks to earlier detection. In bowel and cervical screening, precancerous changes are also removed, **preventing** cancers.
- **False positives**: abnormal results that lead to more tests without cancer being present, with the anxiety they cause.
- **False negatives**: no test finds every cancer.
- **Overdiagnosis**: finding, and treating, tumours that would never have caused problems.

## If you have symptoms, don't wait

Screening is for people without symptoms. **If you notice a symptom** (a breast lump, blood in your stools, abnormal vaginal bleeding), see a doctor even if you were recently screened or your next test is not due yet.

People at **high risk** (because of family history or inherited syndromes) do not follow population screening but specific surveillance.
