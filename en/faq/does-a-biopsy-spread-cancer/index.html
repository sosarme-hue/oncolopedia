FAQ

Do biopsies or surgery spread cancer?

A common fear that delays diagnosis. What studies say about biopsies, surgery and "air" reaching the tumour.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

The short answer

Not in normal practice. The risk of a needle biopsy spreading tumour cells along its track is very low, and doctors take steps to reduce it further, such as planning the needle's path. Delaying or avoiding a biopsy out of fear is dangerous, because without it the type of cancer cannot be known or the right treatment chosen.

Studies of thousands of patients, for example in breast or prostate cancer, have not shown that biopsy worsens survival.

Special cases

In some tumours the biopsy should be done, or at least planned, by the team that will operate, as with sarcomas, so the track can be removed later. In testicular tumours no biopsy is taken through the scrotum. These are precautions specialists know well.

"Air makes cancer spread"

This is an old myth: some people believe that opening the body during an operation and exposing it to air makes cancer spread. It does not. What sometimes happens is that surgery reveals the cancer was more widespread than the tests showed.
