Cancer types

Cancer of unknown primary

Metastases where the original tumour cannot be found despite full investigation. Molecular tests help identify the origin or a treatable target.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common 2-3% of all cancers
What it is Biopsy-confirmed metastases with no identifiable primary tumour
Favourable subgroups 15-20%: treated as a specific tumour, with a good outlook
Treatment By subgroup, molecular profile and suspected origin

How it is investigated

Full examination, blood tests, CT of chest, abdomen and pelvis, PET-CT, mammography in women, endoscopy if there are symptoms, and above all biopsy with immunohistochemistry. The tumour's molecular profile may reveal the likely origin (through expression or methylation patterns) or a treatable alteration.

Favourable subgroups

  • A woman with metastases only in armpit nodes → treated as breast cancer.
  • A woman with serous peritoneal carcinomatosis → as ovarian cancer.
  • Neck nodes with squamous carcinoma (often HPV-positive) → as head and neck cancer.
  • A young man with a midline mass and raised markers → as a germ cell tumour.
  • Well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumours.
  • A single resectable metastasis.

Treatment

Favourable subgroups are treated according to their likely origin. Otherwise, platinum chemotherapy or targeted therapy according to the molecular profile. The CUPISCO trial showed that molecularly guided treatment improves disease control compared with standard chemotherapy.

See also: Biomarkers and molecular profiling, Bone metastases, Brain metastases.
