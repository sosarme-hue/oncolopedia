## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **What they are** | Cancers arising in bone, cartilage, muscle, fat, blood vessels or nerves |
| **How common** | About 1% of adult cancers and around 10-15% of childhood cancers |
| **Types** | Soft tissue (liposarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, GIST…) and bone (osteosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, chondrosarcoma) |
| **Sign** | A growing lump, larger than 5 cm or deep; persistent bone pain |
| **Key point** | Biopsy and treatment in specialist centres |

## What they are

While carcinomas arise from cells lining organs, **sarcomas** arise from **connective tissue**: the tissue that supports and holds the body together. They are rare and very diverse, with more than 70 subtypes in the WHO classification, each with its own behaviour.

- **Soft tissue sarcomas**: in the arms, legs, trunk or abdomen. The most common are liposarcoma, leiomyosarcoma and pleomorphic sarcoma. **Gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST)** arises in the wall of the digestive tract.
- **Bone sarcomas**: **osteosarcoma** and **Ewing sarcoma**, more common in teenagers and young adults, and **chondrosarcoma** in adults.

## Symptoms

- A **lump** that is growing, especially if larger than 5 cm, deep to the fascia or new. It is often painless.
- Persistent **bone pain**, worse at night, sometimes with swelling or a fracture after a minor knock.

A lump with these features needs an **ultrasound** and, if suspicious, referral to a specialist centre **before** any biopsy or surgery.

## Why specialist centres

A poorly planned biopsy or an excision without proper margins (a "whoops procedure") can worsen the outlook or lead to an avoidable amputation. Experienced centres have specialist pathologists, who often use molecular tests to confirm the subtype, and multidisciplinary teams.

## Treatment

- **Surgery** with wide margins, sparing the limb whenever possible.
- **Radiotherapy**, before or after surgery.
- **Chemotherapy** for sensitive subtypes, such as osteosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma or rhabdomyosarcoma.
- **Targeted therapy**: **imatinib** transformed the treatment of GIST, which carries mutations in *KIT* or *PDGFRA*.
