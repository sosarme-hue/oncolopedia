## An immigrant in New York

Georgios Papanikolaou trained as a doctor in Athens and as a zoologist in Germany. In 1913 he emigrated to the United States with his wife, Andromache Mavroyeni (Mary), with no money and no English. He worked as a carpet salesman and a restaurant violinist before getting a post at Cornell University in New York.

## From guinea pigs to cancer

Studying changes in vaginal cells of guinea pigs through the cycle, and later of women, including Mary, who had daily smears for years, he noticed in 1928 that some samples contained **cancer cells**. He presented it at a conference under the title "New cancer diagnosis", to little response.

In 1943 he published a monograph with the gynaecologist Herbert Traut showing that the smear detected cervical cancer, and above all **precancerous changes**, before they caused symptoms. This time it convinced people.

## Screening that saves lives

The **Pap test** spread in the 1950s. In countries with organised programmes, cervical cancer deaths fell by more than 50%. It was the first mass cancer screening test. Today, for many age groups, it is being replaced by the more sensitive **human papillomavirus** test.
