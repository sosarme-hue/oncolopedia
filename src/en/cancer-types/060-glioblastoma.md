## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 3-4 cases per 100,000 people a year; half of all gliomas |
| **Who** | Mainly aged 55 to 75 |
| **Definition** | Adult diffuse glioma, ***IDH*-wildtype**, grade 4 |
| **Treatment** | Maximal safe surgery, radiotherapy with temozolomide (Stupp protocol), tumour treating fields |
| **Survival** | Median 15-20 months; 5-10% live more than 5 years |

## What it is

It arises from glial cells and infiltrates the brain diffusely, so complete removal is impossible: microscopic cells always remain. Since 2021, the WHO defines glioblastoma as an adult diffuse glioma **without an *IDH* mutation**, with necrosis, vascular proliferation or certain molecular alterations (*TERT*, *EGFR*, chromosome 7 gain and 10 loss).

## Symptoms

They develop over weeks: headache, seizures, one-sided weakness, speech problems, confusion, personality changes.

## Treatment

1. **Surgery**: maximal safe resection, with fluorescence (5-ALA), neuronavigation and awake surgery techniques; if not possible, biopsy.
2. **Radiotherapy** (6 weeks) with daily **temozolomide**, followed by 6 cycles of temozolomide (**Stupp** protocol, 2005). In older or frailer people, shorter schedules.
3. **Tumour treating fields (TTFields)**: a portable device on the scalp that improved survival in the EF-14 trial; availability varies by country.
4. ***MGMT*** methylation predicts greater benefit from temozolomide.
5. **Relapse**: repeat surgery, re-irradiation, lomustine, bevacizumab (improves swelling and symptoms), clinical trials.

## Research

Vaccines (dendritic cell, RNA), oncolytic viruses, CAR-T therapies (including dual-target intrathecal ones that showed rapid shrinkage in early studies), *EGFR* inhibitors and new drugs that cross the blood-brain barrier.

## Outlook

Median survival with treatment is about 15-20 months, longer in younger people, with good resection and methylated *MGMT*. Support from neuro-oncology, rehabilitation and palliative care is essential for patients and families.
