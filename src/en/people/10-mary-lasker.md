## An advertiser with a cause

In the 1940s Mary Woodard Lasker and her husband Albert, an advertising executive, decided to devote their fortune and influence to medical research. Mary believed cancer should be treated as a national problem, with public money and an organisation comparable to a large scientific project.

## Transforming a medical society

In 1945 the Laskers reorganised the American Society for the Control of Cancer, a small association of doctors, turning it into the **American Cancer Society**, with public fundraising campaigns and part of the funds set aside for research. They also founded the **Lasker Awards**, now among the most prestigious in medicine.

## The war on cancer

Mary Lasker was the great champion in Washington of the budget of the US **National Cancer Institute**. With Sidney Farber she drove a campaign, including full-page newspaper advertisements, that culminated in the **National Cancer Act** signed by President Nixon in 1971, which multiplied research funding.

## Light and shade

Her strategy of seeking "the cure" within a few years raised expectations that were not met, and some scientists criticised politics for setting the direction of research. But the funding she secured made many of the following decades' advances possible.
