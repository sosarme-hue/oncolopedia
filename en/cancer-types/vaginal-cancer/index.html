Cancer types

Vaginal cancer

One of the rarest gynaecological cancers. It is almost always an HPV-related squamous cell carcinoma and is treated mainly with radiotherapy.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common 1-2% of gynaecological cancers; about 18,000 cases a year worldwide
Who Mostly women over 60
Main cause Persistent HPV infection
Symptoms Bleeding after sex or after menopause, discharge, lump
Treatment External radiotherapy and brachytherapy, with cisplatin; surgery in selected cases

What it is

Primary vaginal cancer is very rare: most tumours found in the vagina come from other organs (cervix, endometrium, vulva, rectum). Types:

  • Squamous cell carcinoma (85-90%), HPV-related and preceded by intraepithelial lesions (VaIN).
  • Adenocarcinoma, including the rare clear cell adenocarcinoma in women exposed before birth to diethylstilbestrol (DES), a drug used in pregnancy between 1940 and 1971.
  • Mucosal melanoma and, in small girls, embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma (botryoid sarcoma).

Risk factors and prevention

HPV, smoking, previous hysterectomy for cervical lesions, pelvic radiotherapy, weakened immunity. The HPV vaccine and follow-up of women with previous lesions prevent most cases.

Symptoms and diagnosis

Abnormal vaginal bleeding (especially after sex or after menopause), watery or smelly discharge, a lump, pain passing urine or in the pelvis. It is diagnosed by colposcopy and biopsy; pelvic MRI and PET-CT define the extent.

Treatment

  • External radiotherapy with brachytherapy, usually with weekly cisplatin, as in cervical cancer. This is the standard treatment in most cases.
  • Surgery for small tumours in the upper vagina.
  • Precursor lesions: laser, excision or imiquimod.
  • Advanced disease: chemotherapy, with immunotherapy in some cases.

Outlook

Five-year survival is about 70% at stage I and around 50% overall, lower in advanced cases. After radiotherapy, vaginal dilators and psychosexual support are important to prevent narrowing.

See also: Cervical cancer, Vulval cancer, Rhabdomyosarcoma.
