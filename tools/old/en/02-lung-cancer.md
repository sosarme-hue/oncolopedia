## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | The most diagnosed cancer worldwide: about 2.5 million cases and 1.8 million deaths in 2022 |
| **Main cause** | Tobacco, in around eight in ten cases |
| **Types** | Non-small cell (about 85%: adenocarcinoma, squamous…) and small cell (about 15%) |
| **Signs** | A persistent or changed cough, coughing up blood, breathlessness, weight loss |
| **Survival** | Low overall (around 15-20% at five years), but much better when found at a localised stage |

## What it is

Lung cancer starts in the cells lining the airways and air sacs. There are two main groups that behave and are treated differently:

- **Non-small cell lung cancer**. **Adenocarcinoma** is the most common, including in people who have never smoked. Others are squamous cell carcinoma and large cell carcinoma.
- **Small cell lung cancer**. It grows quickly, almost always in smokers, and is usually diagnosed once it has spread.

**Mesothelioma** is not a lung cancer but a cancer of the pleura, the membrane around the lungs, and is linked to asbestos.

## Risk factors

**Tobacco** is by far the most important, and the risk falls steadily after quitting at any age. Second-hand smoke, **radon** (a natural radioactive gas that can build up in some homes), asbestos and other workplace exposures, and air pollution also play a part.

## Symptoms

Early on there are usually none. Later, there may be a cough that does not go away or changes, coughing up blood, chest pain, breathlessness, hoarseness, repeated chest infections, tiredness and weight loss.

## Diagnosis

A **CT scan of the chest** is followed by a sample taken by **bronchoscopy**, needle biopsy or surgery. In advanced adenocarcinoma, **molecular biomarkers** (*EGFR*, *ALK*, *ROS1*, *KRAS*, *BRAF* and others) and **PD-L1** are tested because they change treatment. Staging is completed with PET-CT and other tests.

**Low-dose CT screening** for high-risk current and former smokers reduces deaths. It is offered in the United States and the United Kingdom, and the European Union recommends exploring it.

## Treatment

- **Localised disease**: surgery or, if surgery is not possible, **stereotactic radiotherapy**. Treatment before or after with chemotherapy, immunotherapy or targeted drugs is often added.
- **Locally advanced disease**: chemotherapy combined with radiotherapy, often followed by immunotherapy.
- **Advanced disease**: if there is a treatable alteration, **targeted therapies** in tablet form (for example against *EGFR* or *ALK*), which can control the disease for years. If not, **immunotherapy**, alone or with chemotherapy.
- **Small cell** cancer is treated with chemotherapy and immunotherapy, and radiotherapy in selected cases.

## Outlook

It depends mainly on stage, subtype, molecular changes and general health. Figures for non-small cell cancer cannot be applied to small cell cancer or mesothelioma. Over the last fifteen years, immunotherapy and targeted therapies have brought long survival that used to be exceptional.
