## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 5-6% of childhood cancers |
| **Who** | Children aged 2 to 5 |
| **Sign** | A painless **abdominal lump**, often noticed at bath time; blood in the urine, high blood pressure |
| **Syndromes** | WAGR, Denys-Drash, Beckwith-Wiedemann, hemihypertrophy |
| **Treatment** | Chemotherapy and surgery (nephrectomy); radiotherapy for some |
| **Cure** | Over 90% |

## Treatment

In Europe (**SIOP-RTSG UMBRELLA** protocol) **chemotherapy is given before surgery** (vincristine and actinomycin D) to shrink the tumour and reduce the risk of rupture; then **nephrectomy** and further treatment according to histology and stage. In North America (COG) surgery comes first. For bilateral tumours, kidney tissue is preserved where possible.

## Follow-up

Monitoring of the remaining kidney's function, blood pressure and late effects of chemotherapy and radiotherapy; ultrasound screening in children with predisposing syndromes until age 7-8.
