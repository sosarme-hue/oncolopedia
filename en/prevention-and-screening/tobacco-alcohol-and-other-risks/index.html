Prevention and screening

Tobacco, alcohol and other risks

Avoidable cancer risk factors, ranked by how much they really matter. And why something being carcinogenic is not the same as how dangerous it is.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

Tobacco comes first

Tobacco is the leading avoidable cause of cancer. It causes most lung cancers and also cancers of the mouth, pharynx, larynx, oesophagus, stomach, pancreas, liver, kidney, bladder and cervix, and acute myeloid leukaemia. There is no safe level, and second-hand smoke also raises risk.

Stopping smoking helps at any age: lung cancer risk falls steadily in the following years. Family doctors, stop-smoking services and medicines help people succeed. E-cigarettes are not harmless and are not recommended for non-smokers.

Alcohol

Alcohol is carcinogenic in any form (wine, beer or spirits). It increases the risk of cancers of the mouth, pharynx, larynx, oesophagus, liver, bowel and breast, even at moderate levels. The risk rises with the amount. Combined with tobacco, it multiplies the risk of head and neck cancers.

Weight, diet and physical activity

Obesity is linked to at least thirteen types of cancer, including endometrial, oesophageal (adenocarcinoma), kidney, pancreatic, liver, bowel and postmenopausal breast cancer. Physical activity reduces the risk of bowel, breast and endometrial cancer. Processed meat (ham, bacon, sausages) raises the risk of bowel cancer, and fibre lowers it.

Infections

Infections cause about 13% of cancers worldwide: HPV, hepatitis B and C viruses, the bacterium Helicobacter pylori, Epstein-Barr virus and HIV (through immune suppression). Vaccines, antiviral treatment and eradicating H. pylori prevent them.

Radiation and environment

  • Ultraviolet radiation from the sun and sunbeds: melanoma and other skin cancers.
  • Radon: a natural radioactive gas, the second cause of lung cancer after tobacco. Home levels can be measured and reduced.
  • Air pollution: classified by IARC as carcinogenic to the lung.
  • Workplace exposures: asbestos, silica, benzene, diesel fumes and others. They should be controlled through safety rules.
  • X-rays and scans use low doses justified by their medical benefit.

Hazard is not the same as risk

IARC groups agents by the strength of the evidence that they can cause cancer, not by how much they increase it. Tobacco and processed meat are both in Group 1, but smoking multiplies lung cancer risk many times over, while 50 grams of processed meat a day raises bowel cancer risk by about 18%. To make decisions, the size of the risk is what matters.
