Cancer types

Endometrial cancer

The most common gynaecological cancer in high-income countries. It usually gives an early warning with bleeding after menopause, and most cases are cured with surgery.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common About 420,000 cases worldwide in 2022
Who Mostly women after menopause
Typical sign Any vaginal bleeding after menopause
Risks Obesity, excess oestrogen, diabetes, Lynch syndrome
Survival Around 75-80% at five years; over 90% at stage I

What it is

This is cancer of the endometrium, the lining of the womb. It is not the same as cervical cancer or uterine sarcomas, which are much rarer.

It is now also classified by its molecular profile, which has been built into staging and helps guide treatment: tumours with POLE mutations (very good outlook), with mismatch repair deficiency (sometimes linked to Lynch syndrome), with abnormal p53 (more aggressive) and with no specific profile.

Risk factors

Exposure of the endometrium to oestrogen without enough progesterone: obesity (fat tissue makes oestrogen), late menopause, never having been pregnant, some hormone treatments and tamoxifen. Also diabetes and Lynch syndrome. The contraceptive pill and physical activity reduce the risk.

Symptoms

Any bleeding after menopause should be checked, however slight. In younger women, very heavy periods or persistent bleeding between periods. Because it gives an early warning, most cases are diagnosed at an early stage.

Diagnosis

Transvaginal ultrasound, which measures endometrial thickness, and an endometrial biopsy, in clinic or by hysteroscopy, followed by staging tests.

Treatment

  • Surgery: removal of the womb, tubes and ovaries, usually by keyhole or robotic surgery, with lymph node assessment (often by sentinel node).
  • Radiotherapy (mainly vaginal brachytherapy) and chemotherapy depending on relapse risk.
  • In advanced or recurrent disease, immunotherapy with chemotherapy, especially effective in mismatch repair-deficient tumours, and hormone therapy in some cases.
  • Young women with very early tumours who wish to have children may be offered hormone treatment that preserves the womb, with close follow-up.
