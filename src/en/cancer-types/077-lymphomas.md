## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | Non-Hodgkin: 550,000 cases a year worldwide; Hodgkin: 80,000 |
| **Typical sign** | Large, painless **lymph nodes** in the neck, armpits or groin |
| **B symptoms** | Unexplained fever, heavy **night sweats**, loss of more than 10% of body weight |
| **Diagnosis** | **Biopsy of a whole node**; PET-CT |
| **Treatment** | Immunochemotherapy (rituximab), radiotherapy, CAR-T, bispecific antibodies, monitoring for indolent types |

## What they are

The **lymphatic system** includes lymph nodes, spleen, thymus, bone marrow and lymphoid tissue in many organs. Lymphomas arise from **B, T or NK lymphocytes** at some stage of maturation.

## Practical classification

| Group | Examples | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Hodgkin lymphoma** | Classic, nodular lymphocyte-predominant | Very curable |
| **Aggressive B-cell non-Hodgkin** | [Diffuse large B-cell](../diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma/), [Burkitt](../burkitt-lymphoma/), [mantle cell](../mantle-cell-lymphoma/) | Grow fast; many cured |
| **Indolent B-cell non-Hodgkin** | [Follicular](../follicular-lymphoma/), [marginal zone](../marginal-zone-lymphomas/), small lymphocytic, [Waldenström](../waldenstrom-macroglobulinaemia/) | Slow; controlled for years |
| **T and NK lymphomas** | [Peripheral T-cell](../t-cell-lymphomas/), [cutaneous](../cutaneous-lymphomas/) | Varied; many aggressive |
| **Others** | [Primary CNS lymphoma](../primary-cns-lymphoma/) | |

[Multiple myeloma](../multiple-myeloma/) and [chronic lymphocytic leukaemia](../chronic-lymphocytic-leukaemia/) are also B-cell neoplasms.

## Risk factors

- **Weakened immunity**: HIV, transplants, immunosuppressive drugs.
- **Infections**: Epstein-Barr virus (Burkitt, Hodgkin), *Helicobacter pylori* (gastric MALT), hepatitis C (marginal zone), HTLV-1 (adult T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma), HHV-8.
- **Autoimmune diseases**: Sjögren syndrome, rheumatoid arthritis, coeliac disease (intestinal T-cell lymphoma).
- Pesticides and solvents, family history, age.
- **Textured breast implants**: implant-associated anaplastic lymphoma (rare).

## Symptoms

Painless **nodes** growing over weeks, tiredness, **B symptoms**, generalised itching (Hodgkin), pain after drinking alcohol (Hodgkin, rare), enlarged spleen, cough or breathlessness from chest masses.

## Diagnosis

1. Surgical or core needle **biopsy** of a node: fine needle aspiration is not enough.
2. Immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry and genetic studies.
3. **PET-CT** for staging (Lugano) and response assessment.
4. Blood tests (LDH, beta-2 microglobulin), serology (HIV, hepatitis), sometimes bone marrow biopsy.

## Stages (Lugano)

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| **I** | One node region |
| **II** | Two or more regions on the same side of the diaphragm |
| **III** | Nodes on both sides of the diaphragm |
| **IV** | Diffuse organ involvement (liver, marrow, lung) |

## Treatment

- **Immunochemotherapy**: **rituximab** (anti-CD20 antibody, 1997) was the first monoclonal antibody approved against cancer and improved cure rates in B-cell lymphomas.
- **Radiotherapy** for localised stages.
- **Watch and wait** for indolent lymphomas without symptoms.
- **Targeted therapy**: BTK inhibitors, lenalidomide, tazemetostat, brentuximab vedotin, polatuzumab vedotin.
- **CAR-T** (axicabtagene, lisocabtagene, tisagenlecleucel) and **bispecific antibodies** (glofitamab, epcoritamab, mosunetuzumab) at relapse, increasingly earlier.
- **Autologous or allogeneic transplant** for selected relapses.

## Outlook

Over 85-90% of Hodgkin lymphomas and 60-70% of diffuse large B-cell lymphomas are cured. Indolent types are not usually cured, but many people live for decades with good quality of life.
