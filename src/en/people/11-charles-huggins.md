## Dogs and prostates

Charles Huggins, a urologist at the University of Chicago, studied the fluid produced by the prostate of dogs. He saw that the gland shrank when the testicles were removed or oestrogens were given, and grew again with testosterone. He thought prostate tumours might keep that dependence on hormones.

## A cancer that obeys hormones

In 1941, with Clarence Hodges, he reported that in men with **metastatic prostate cancer**, **castration** or **oestrogens** lowered acid phosphatase in the blood and relieved the pain of bone metastases, sometimes dramatically. It was the first time an advanced cancer had been controlled by systemic treatment, without surgery on the tumour or radiation.

## The basis of hormone therapy

His work showed that some cancers need hormones to grow and that depriving them of hormones holds them back. It is the foundation of **hormone therapy** for prostate and breast cancer, which today saves hundreds of thousands of lives each year. He received the Nobel Prize in 1966, shared with Peyton Rous.
