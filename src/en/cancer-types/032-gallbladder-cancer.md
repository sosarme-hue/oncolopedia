## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 120,000 cases a year worldwide; very high rates in Chile, Bolivia and northern India |
| **Who** | More in women (2-3 times) and over-65s |
| **Risk factors** | Long-standing gallstones, porcelain gallbladder, polyps > 1 cm, obesity |
| **Finding** | One in three is found when a gallbladder removed for stones is examined |
| **Treatment** | Extended cholecystectomy; chemotherapy and immunotherapy as for cholangiocarcinoma |

## What it is

The **gallbladder** stores bile under the liver. Its cancer is an **adenocarcinoma** in 90%. It is linked to **chronic inflammation** from stones. In Chile it is a leading cause of cancer death in women, and removing gallbladders with stones at a young age is recommended there as prevention.

## Prevention

Removing the gallbladder for **polyps of 1 cm or more**, porcelain gallbladder or symptomatic stones.

## Symptoms and diagnosis

Early ones cause no symptoms. Later: right-sided pain, jaundice, weight loss, a mass. Ultrasound, CT, MRI, CA 19-9 and CEA.

## Treatment

- **Found after cholecystectomy**: if the tumour invades beyond the mucosa (T1b or more), re-operation removes part of the liver and the nodes (**extended cholecystectomy**).
- **Capecitabine** after surgery.
- Advanced disease: gemcitabine and cisplatin with durvalumab or pembrolizumab; targeted therapy (especially **HER2**, amplified in 15-20%).
- Biliary drainage and palliative care.

## Outlook

Excellent for early incidental findings (over 80% for T1); poor for advanced disease (5-10% at five years).
