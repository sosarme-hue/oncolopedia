## A right, not a sign of distrust

Most health systems recognise patients' right to information, to decide about their own treatment and to seek a **second opinion**, especially for serious illnesses such as cancer. Good professionals understand this and provide reports and test results.

## When it can help

- A **rare** cancer or a difficult diagnosis, such as some sarcomas or lymphomas.
- When very different treatment options are being considered, or very complex surgery.
- If the situation has not been explained well or you are unsure about the decision.
- To explore the possibility of a **clinical trial**.

For most common cancers, treatment is decided by a **multidisciplinary team** following clinical guidelines, and a second opinion usually confirms the plan. Asking for one should not delay urgent treatment.

## How to do it

1. Ask your team for a copy of your **medical records**, the pathology report and imaging.
2. Ask your doctor or health service how to request a second opinion, or go to a specialist centre.
3. Sometimes it is enough for another expert pathologist to review the samples.
