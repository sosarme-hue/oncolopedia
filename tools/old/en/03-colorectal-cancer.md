## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | About 1.9 million cases worldwide in 2022, the third most common cancer |
| **Origin** | Most start as a polyp (adenoma) that takes years to become malignant |
| **Screening** | Stool tests (such as FIT) every one or two years, or colonoscopy, depending on the country |
| **Signs** | Blood in the stool, a lasting change in bowel habit, unexplained anaemia |
| **Survival** | Around 60-65% at five years in Europe; over 90% at stage I |

## What it is

This is cancer of the colon and rectum, the last part of the bowel. Almost all are **adenocarcinomas** that develop slowly from **polyps**. That slowness is an opportunity: if a polyp is found and removed, the cancer never develops.

## Risk factors

Age, family history, inherited syndromes (such as **Lynch syndrome** or familial adenomatous polyposis), long-standing inflammatory bowel disease, obesity, inactivity, smoking, alcohol and high intake of **red and processed meat**. A fibre-rich diet and physical activity lower the risk. The rise in cases in people under 50 is a concern, and its causes are not fully understood.

## Symptoms

- Blood in the stool or very dark stools.
- A change in bowel habit lasting weeks (diarrhoea, constipation, narrower stools).
- A feeling of not emptying the bowel fully.
- Abdominal pain, tiredness, weight loss or **iron-deficiency anaemia** with no other explanation.

## Diagnosis

The key test is **colonoscopy**, which shows the whole colon and allows biopsies and polyp removal. Staging uses CT and, for the rectum, MRI. The tumour is tested for **microsatellite instability** or mismatch repair proteins (which can point to Lynch syndrome and guide treatment) and, in advanced disease, for genes such as *KRAS*, *NRAS* and *BRAF*.

## Treatment

- **Colon**: surgery to remove the affected segment with its lymph nodes. If nodes are involved, adjuvant chemotherapy.
- **Rectum**: often radiotherapy and chemotherapy before surgery. In some people with a complete response, a "watch and wait" approach without surgery is possible in experienced centres.
- **Advanced disease**: chemotherapy combined with antibodies (anti-EGFR or anti-angiogenic, depending on biology). Liver or lung metastases can sometimes be removed with curative intent. Tumours with microsatellite instability respond very well to **immunotherapy**.

## Outlook

Stage is the main factor. Screening has allowed more cases to be found early and has reduced deaths in countries with good participation.
