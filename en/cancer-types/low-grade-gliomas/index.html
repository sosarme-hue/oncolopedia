Cancer types

Low-grade gliomas (IDH-mutant astrocytoma and oligodendroglioma)

Slow-growing gliomas in young adults, defined by the IDH mutation. Vorasidenib is the first targeted therapy to delay their progression.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
Who Adults aged 25 to 45
Types IDH-mutant astrocytoma (grades 2-4) and IDH-mutant, 1p/19q-codeleted oligodendroglioma (grades 2-3)
Typical symptom Seizures
Treatment Surgery; surveillance, vorasidenib, or radiotherapy with chemotherapy (PCV or temozolomide)
Survival Median over 10 years for grade 2 astrocytoma and 15-20 years for oligodendroglioma

What they are

They are diffuse gliomas carrying a mutation of the IDH1 or IDH2 gene, which produces an abnormal metabolite (2-hydroxyglutarate). They grow slowly over years but eventually progress to higher grades.

  • Oligodendroglioma: with 1p/19q codeletion; the best outlook and most chemotherapy-sensitive.
  • Astrocytoma: no codeletion, often with ATRX and TP53 mutations. CDKN2A/B deletion makes it grade 4.

Treatment

  • Surgery as complete as possible, often awake.
  • After surgery, in low risk: MRI surveillance or vorasidenib (an IDH1/2 inhibitor which, in the INDIGO trial, more than doubled time to progression and delays the need for radiotherapy).
  • In high risk or at progression: radiotherapy followed by PCV (procarbazine, lomustine, vincristine) or temozolomide, which greatly prolong survival (RTOG 9802 and CATNON trials).
  • Seizure control and care for cognitive function.

See also: Brain tumours, Glioblastoma.
