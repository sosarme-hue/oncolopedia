## From Warsaw to Paris

Maria Skłodowska was born in Warsaw, then under Russian rule, where women could not attend university. She studied in secret and in 1891 moved to Paris, where she took degrees in physics and mathematics. There she met Pierre Curie, whom she married in 1895.

## Radioactivity

Inspired by Henri Becquerel's discovery that uranium gave off rays, Marie studied the phenomenon, which she named **radioactivity**. In 1898, with Pierre, she discovered two new elements: **polonium**, named after her country, and **radium**. In 1903 they shared the Nobel Prize in Physics with Becquerel; Marie was the first woman to receive one. In 1911 she won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry alone. She is the only person honoured in two different sciences.

## From laboratory to clinic

It was soon seen that radium burned skin and destroyed tissue, and that it could shrink tumours. This gave rise to **Curietherapy**, now called **brachytherapy**. The **Radium Institute** in Paris, which she directed, had a medical research section that became a pioneering centre of radiotherapy. During the First World War she organised mobile X-ray units, the "petites Curies", to treat the wounded.

## The price

The Curies handled radioactive materials without protection for years. Marie died in 1934 of aplastic anaemia, probably caused by radiation. Her notebooks are still radioactive and are kept in lead-lined boxes.
