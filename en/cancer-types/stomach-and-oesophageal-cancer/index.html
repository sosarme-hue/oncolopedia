Cancer types

Stomach and oesophageal cancer

Two upper digestive cancers with different causes: the bacterium Helicobacter pylori in the stomach, and tobacco, alcohol and reflux in the oesophagus.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common Stomach: about 970,000 cases worldwide in 2022, the fifth most common. Oesophagus: about 510,000
Main causes Stomach: Helicobacter pylori, salty diet, smoking. Oesophagus: smoking and alcohol (squamous); reflux and obesity (adenocarcinoma)
Signs Difficulty swallowing, persistent indigestion, vomiting, anaemia, weight loss
Key test Upper endoscopy (gastroscopy) with biopsy

Stomach cancer

Almost all are adenocarcinomas. Rates have fallen sharply in Europe thanks to refrigeration (fewer salted and smoked foods) and less infection with Helicobacter pylori, a bacterium classified by IARC as carcinogenic. It remains very common in East Asia and parts of Latin America.

Symptoms appear late: upper abdominal discomfort, feeling full quickly, nausea, anaemia and weight loss. Persistent digestive symptoms in someone over 50-55, or with warning signs, call for a gastroscopy.

Localised disease is treated with chemotherapy before and after surgery (gastrectomy). In advanced disease, HER2, PD-L1, claudin 18.2 and microsatellite instability are tested, because they point to targeted therapy or immunotherapy.

Treating H. pylori infection lowers the risk, and some high-incidence countries screen with endoscopy.

Oesophageal cancer

There are two types with different causes:

  • Squamous cell carcinoma: in the middle and upper oesophagus, linked mainly to smoking and alcohol, and to very hot drinks.
  • Adenocarcinoma: in the lower oesophagus, near the stomach, linked to long-standing reflux, obesity and Barrett's oesophagus, a change in the lining that is monitored with endoscopy.

The typical symptom is dysphagia: difficulty swallowing, first solids and then liquids, with weight loss. Localised disease is usually treated with chemoradiotherapy and surgery (oesophagectomy), sometimes followed by immunotherapy. Advanced disease is treated with chemotherapy, immunotherapy and measures to keep swallowing possible, such as stents.

Outlook

It depends on stage. Found early, some tumours can even be removed endoscopically; once they cause symptoms they tend to be more advanced.
