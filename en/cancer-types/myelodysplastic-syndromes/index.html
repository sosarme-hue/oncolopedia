Cancer types

Myelodysplastic syndromes

Bone marrow diseases that make faulty blood cells, mainly in older people. They cause anaemia and can progress to leukaemia.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
Current name Myelodysplastic neoplasms (WHO 2022)
How common 4-5 cases per 100,000 a year; over 20 per 100,000 in over-70s
Symptoms Anaemia not responding to iron or vitamins, infections, bruising
Outlook Calculated with the IPSS-M score, which includes mutations
Treatment Transfusions, erythropoietin, luspatercept, lenalidomide, azacitidine, transplant

What they are

A marrow stem cell acquires mutations (SF3B1, TET2, ASXL1, TP53...) and produces dysplastic (abnormally shaped) cells that do not work properly. The result is cytopenias (low blood counts). About 30% progress to acute myeloid leukaemia.

Risk factors: age, previous chemotherapy or radiotherapy, benzene, tobacco. Its precursor is clonal haematopoiesis, very common with age.

Treatment by risk

Lower risk (aim: improve anaemia and quality of life): - Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents. - Luspatercept (especially with SF3B1 or ring sideroblasts) and imetelstat. - Lenalidomide for 5q deletion. - Transfusions and iron chelation.

Higher risk (aim: prolong life): - Azacitidine or decitabine. - Allogeneic transplant, the only curative treatment, in fit patients.

See also: Acute myeloid leukaemia, Myeloproliferative neoplasms, Leukaemias.
