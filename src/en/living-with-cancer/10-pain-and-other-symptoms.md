## Supportive care

**Supportive care** means treating the symptoms of the cancer and the side effects of treatment, at every stage, not only at the end. It includes pain control, nutrition, rehabilitation, psychological care and social support. **Palliative care** teams specialise in it; involving them early, alongside cancer treatment, improves quality of life and, in some studies, even survival. See [Palliative care](../../treatments/palliative-care/).

## Pain

Pain is common, but in most cases it can be controlled. To treat it well, your team needs to know:

- **Where** it hurts and whether it spreads.
- **How much**: on a scale from 0 (none) to 10 (the worst imaginable).
- **What it is like**: aching, burning, electric shocks, cramping.
- **When**: constant, at night, with movement, in sudden flares.
- What makes it better or worse, and what you have already taken.

Treatment is chosen by intensity and type:

| Pain | Usual options |
|---|---|
| **Mild** | Paracetamol, anti-inflammatories (with care) |
| **Moderate to severe** | Opioids (morphine, oxycodone, fentanyl and others), at fixed times, plus extra doses for flares |
| **Nerve pain** | Specific drugs (gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine) |
| **Bone metastases** | Radiotherapy, drugs that strengthen bone |
| **Difficult cases** | Nerve blocks and other techniques in pain units |

**Common myths**:
- *"Morphine means the end is near."* No: it is used at any stage when pain is significant.
- *"I will get addicted."* When used for cancer pain under medical supervision, addiction is uncommon. Physical tolerance is normal and is managed by adjusting the dose.
- *"It's better to hold out."* Untreated pain worsens sleep, mood, appetite and mobility.

Opioids cause **constipation** almost always: take a laxative from the start. Drowsiness and nausea usually improve within a few days.

## Other common symptoms

- **Tiredness (fatigue)**: the most frequent. Physical exercise adapted to you is what helps most. Rule out anaemia, thyroid problems or depression.
- **Loss of appetite and weight**: small, frequent meals rich in protein; a dietitian can help. See [Diet and exercise](../diet-and-exercise/).
- **Nausea**: there are very effective preventive medicines. Ask if you still have it.
- **Insomnia**: sleep routines; cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia works.
- **Breathlessness**: treat the cause, a fan towards the face, breathing techniques.
- **Anxiety and low mood**: see [What is psycho-oncology](../../psycho-oncology/what-is-psycho-oncology/).

## How to ask for help

Keep a **symptom diary** for a few days before your appointment. Do not wait for the team to ask: say how you feel and how much it limits you. If a symptom is severe or new, check [When to call your team during treatment](../when-to-call-your-team-during-treatment/).
