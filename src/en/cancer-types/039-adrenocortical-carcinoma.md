## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 0.5-2 cases per million people a year |
| **Who** | Two peaks: children under 5 and adults aged 40-50; more in women |
| **Inheritance** | Li-Fraumeni (*TP53*), Lynch, Beckwith-Wiedemann, MEN1 |
| **Symptoms** | Cushing syndrome, virilisation, abdominal mass |
| **Treatment** | Complete surgery; mitotane; EDP chemotherapy |

## What it is

The **adrenal glands**, on top of each kidney, have a **cortex** (making cortisol, aldosterone and androgens) and a **medulla** (adrenaline). Adrenocortical carcinoma arises in the cortex. It must be distinguished from **incidentalomas**, very common adrenal nodules, almost always benign (adenomas), found in 3-7% of CT scans.

Tumours of the medulla are [phaeochromocytomas](../phaeochromocytoma-and-paraganglioma/).

## Symptoms

More than half produce hormones:

- **Cortisol**: rapidly developing Cushing syndrome (weight gain on the trunk, round face, muscle weakness, diabetes, high blood pressure).
- **Androgens**: excess hair, deep voice, acne and menstrual changes in women; early puberty in children.
- Others: abdominal pain or mass due to size, often over 6-10 cm.

## Diagnosis

CT or MRI (a large, heterogeneous, high-density nodule suggests malignancy), full hormone work-up before surgery, and ruling out phaeochromocytoma. Biopsy is not recommended if operable, because of the risk of spread.

## Treatment

- **Complete adrenalectomy** without rupturing the tumour, by an expert surgeon: the main prognostic factor.
- **Mitotane** (a drug that destroys the adrenal cortex) after surgery for high risk; requires cortisol replacement.
- Advanced disease: **EDP** chemotherapy (etoposide, doxorubicin, cisplatin) with mitotane; surgery for selected metastases; radiotherapy.
- Genetic testing, especially for *TP53*, in all children and young adults.

## Outlook

Five-year survival is 60-80% at stages I-II and under 15% at stage IV. Because it is rare, treatment in **reference centres** and networks such as ENSAT is recommended.
