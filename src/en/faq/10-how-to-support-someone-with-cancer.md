## Being there matters more than getting it right

There are no perfect words. What many people with cancer value most is that others **don't disappear**. A message, a short visit or a call is worth more than silence for fear of saying the wrong thing.

## What tends to help

- **Listening** without rushing and letting the person decide what to talk about, including things other than cancer.
- Asking "how are you today?" rather than "how are you?".
- Offering **specific help**: "I'll drive you to hospital on Tuesday", "I'll bring dinner on Thursday", "I'll have the children on Saturday".
- Respecting their decisions about treatment and about whom to tell what.
- Staying in touch **after** treatment too, when a low often comes.

## What to avoid

- Phrases like "you have to be strong" or "everything happens for a reason", which can make people feel guilty for being sad.
- Telling stories of other people with cancer who did badly.
- Recommending miracle diets or alternative therapies.
- Insisting on "positive thinking" as if it cured.

## And look after yourself

Supporting someone is tiring. Carers need rest, information and support too. Cancer charities offer help for families, and patient organisations run groups for carers.
