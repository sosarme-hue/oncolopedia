## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 120,000 cases a year; up to 20-30 per 100,000 in southern China, rare in Europe |
| **Causes** | **Epstein-Barr virus**, genetic predisposition, salted fish in childhood |
| **Signs** | Neck lump, one-sided blocked or bleeding nose, one-sided glue ear in an adult |
| **Treatment** | Radiotherapy (IMRT) with chemotherapy; immunotherapy (toripalimab, camrelizumab) |

## What it is

The **nasopharynx** lies behind the nasal cavity, above the soft palate. Its cancer is almost always a non-keratinising carcinoma associated with **Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)**. It has a very marked geography (Guangdong, Hong Kong, Malaysia, North Africa, Inuit populations).

## Diagnosis

Nasal endoscopy and biopsy, **MRI** of the skull base and neck, PET-CT, and **blood EBV DNA**, useful for prognosis, follow-up and screening in high-incidence areas.

## Treatment

- Surgery is not usually possible because of its location.
- **Intensity-modulated radiotherapy** alone for stage I.
- Cisplatin **chemoradiotherapy** for stages II-IV; for advanced disease, **induction chemotherapy** (gemcitabine and cisplatin) first, which improves survival.
- **Recurrent or metastatic**: gemcitabine and cisplatin with **toripalimab**, camrelizumab or tislelizumab.

## Outlook

Five-year survival exceeds 80% overall with modern treatment, and 90-95% for stages I-II.
