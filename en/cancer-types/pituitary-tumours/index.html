Cancer types

Pituitary tumours

Almost always benign tumours of the gland that directs the body's hormones. They can cause acromegaly, Cushing's, period changes or loss of vision.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common 10-15% of intracranial tumours; small incidental findings very common
Current name Pituitary neuroendocrine tumours (PitNETs), formerly adenomas
Types Prolactinoma, somatotroph (acromegaly), corticotroph (Cushing's), non-functioning
Symptoms Hormone disturbances or loss of side vision from pressure on the optic chiasm
Treatment Drugs (cabergoline for prolactinomas), transsphenoidal surgery, radiosurgery

Types

Tumour Hormone Symptoms
Prolactinoma Prolactin Missed periods, breast milk, infertility, impotence
Somatotroph Growth hormone Acromegaly: enlarging hands, feet and facial features; gigantism in children
Corticotroph ACTH Cushing's disease
Thyrotroph TSH Overactive thyroid (rare)
Non-functioning None Vision loss, headache, hormone deficiency

Treatment

  • Prolactinomas: oral cabergoline, which shrinks them in over 80% without surgery.
  • Others: transsphenoidal surgery (through the nose, endoscopically) by expert neurosurgeons.
  • Drugs for acromegaly (somatostatin analogues, pegvisomant) and Cushing's (osilodrostat, pasireotide).
  • Radiosurgery or radiotherapy for remnants or recurrence.
  • Replacing missing hormones; lifelong endocrine follow-up.

See also: Brain tumours, Meningioma.
