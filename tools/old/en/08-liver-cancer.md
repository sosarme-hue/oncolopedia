## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | About 870,000 cases worldwide in 2022, the sixth most common and the third leading cause of cancer death |
| **Most common type** | Hepatocellular carcinoma. Cholangiocarcinoma starts in the bile ducts |
| **Causes** | Hepatitis B and C, alcohol, fatty liver linked to obesity and diabetes, aflatoxins |
| **Prevention** | Hepatitis B vaccine, hepatitis C treatment, less alcohol |
| **Surveillance** | Ultrasound every six months in people with cirrhosis |

## What it is

**Hepatocellular carcinoma** starts in hepatocytes, the liver's main cells. In over 80% of cases it arises in a liver with **cirrhosis**, that is, scarred by years of disease. **Cholangiocarcinoma** starts in the bile ducts and behaves differently.

It should not be confused with **liver metastases**, which are far more common and come from other cancers, such as colorectal cancer.

## Causes and prevention

- **Hepatitis B**: the vaccine, part of childhood schedules, was the first vaccine to prevent a cancer.
- **Hepatitis C**: now cured with tablets in a few weeks, which lowers the risk.
- **Alcohol** and **metabolic dysfunction-associated liver disease** (fatty liver from obesity and diabetes), a growing cause.
- **Aflatoxins**, fungal toxins that contaminate poorly stored food in some parts of the world.

## Symptoms and diagnosis

It causes no symptoms early on, which is why people with cirrhosis have an **ultrasound every six months**. Later there may be right-sided pain, weight loss, jaundice or worsening cirrhosis. Diagnosis can often be made with contrast **CT or MRI**, because hepatocellular carcinoma has a characteristic pattern; in other cases a biopsy is needed. Blood **alpha-fetoprotein** helps but is not enough.

## Treatment

Treatment depends on the size and number of tumours, liver function and general health (BCLC system):

- **Early stages**: surgery, **liver transplant** or **ablation** (destroying the tumour with heat through a needle).
- **Intermediate stage**: **chemoembolisation**, which delivers chemotherapy through the hepatic artery and blocks the tumour's vessels.
- **Advanced stage**: combination immunotherapy (for example atezolizumab with bevacizumab, or durvalumab with tremelimumab) or other drugs.

## Outlook

It depends as much on the cancer as on how damaged the liver is. When found through surveillance, it can often be treated with curative intent.
