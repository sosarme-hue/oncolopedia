## The first human oncogene

Mariano Barbacid earned his PhD in chemistry at the Complutense University of Madrid and in 1974 moved to the US National Cancer Institute. There his group searched for genes able to turn normal cells into cancer cells.

In 1982, almost at the same time as the groups of Robert Weinberg and Michael Wigler, his team isolated an **oncogene** from a human bladder tumour: an altered version of the **HRAS** gene. That same year they identified the cause of its activation: a **point mutation**, a change in **a single letter** of DNA, which altered one amino acid of the protein.

It was the first proof that a single mutation in a human gene could contribute to cancer.

## The RAS family

**RAS** genes (*KRAS*, *HRAS*, *NRAS*) are mutated in about a fifth of human cancers, and *KRAS* in more than 90% of pancreatic cancers. For decades they were considered "undruggable". In recent years the first *KRAS* G12C inhibitors have arrived, and others against more variants are being studied.

## Return to Spain

In 1998 he returned to set up and lead the **Spanish National Cancer Research Centre (CNIO)** in Madrid, until 2011. His group has continued to study *KRAS*-driven lung and pancreatic cancer models, and has published combined-blockade strategies that eliminate pancreatic tumours in mice, experimental results that still have to be tested in people.
