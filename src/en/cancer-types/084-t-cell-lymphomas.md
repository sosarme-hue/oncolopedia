## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | 10-15% of non-Hodgkin lymphomas in the West; more in Asia |
| **Types** | Peripheral T-cell NOS, angioimmunoblastic, anaplastic (ALK+ and ALK−), extranodal NK/T nasal type, enteropathy-associated, hepatosplenic, adult T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma |
| **Treatment** | CHOP or CHOEP; **brentuximab with CHP** for CD30+; autologous transplant in first remission for some |

## Key points

- **ALK-positive anaplastic lymphoma**, typical of young people, has a good outlook.
- **Breast implant-associated anaplastic lymphoma** (mainly textured implants) is almost always cured by removing the implant and capsule.
- **Extranodal NK/T nasal type** is linked to Epstein-Barr virus and treated with radiotherapy and asparaginase.
- **Enteropathy-associated** lymphoma complicates poorly controlled coeliac disease.

## Treatment

**CHOP** or CHOEP chemotherapy; **brentuximab vedotin with CHP** for CD30+ tumours (ECHELON-2 trial); consolidation autologous transplant for selected patients; at relapse, belinostat, pralatrexate, romidepsin, allogeneic transplant and clinical trials.
