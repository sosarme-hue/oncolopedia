Cancer types

Marginal zone lymphomas

Indolent lymphomas often linked to infection or chronic inflammation. Localised gastric MALT lymphoma with Helicobacter pylori can disappear with antibiotics alone in many cases.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common 5-10% of non-Hodgkin lymphomas
Types Extranodal (MALT), splenic and nodal
Associated causes H. pylori (stomach), hepatitis C (splenic), Sjögren (salivary glands), Hashimoto thyroiditis, Chlamydia psittaci (orbit)
Treatment Treat the infection; radiotherapy; rituximab; BTK inhibitors

Types

  • MALT: in mucous membranes; the most common is gastric. In H. pylori-positive cases, antibiotic eradication cures most.
  • Splenic: large spleen and lymphocytes in the blood; splenectomy or rituximab; antivirals if hepatitis C is present.
  • Nodal: treated like follicular lymphoma.

Treatment

Low-dose radiotherapy for localised disease; rituximab alone or with chemotherapy; zanubrutinib and other BTK inhibitors at relapse.

See also: Lymphomas, Follicular lymphoma, Stomach and oesophageal cancer.
