## Response to treatment

- **Complete remission** (or complete response): tests find no signs of cancer. Very good news, although disease may remain in undetectable amounts.
- **Partial remission** (or partial response): the cancer has shrunk substantially, for example by more than 30% in the size of measurable tumours.
- **Stable disease**: it is neither growing nor shrinking significantly. In advanced disease, keeping cancer stable with good quality of life is a worthwhile goal.
- **Progression**: the cancer grows or new lesions appear.

## Minimal residual disease

In leukaemias, lymphomas and myelomas, very sensitive techniques can detect one malignant cell among a hundred thousand or a million. A negative **minimal residual disease** result indicates a very deep response and a better outlook. In solid tumours it is being studied using tumour DNA in the blood.

## When can we talk about cure?

There is no single moment. Doctors tend to be cautious and say **"no evidence of disease"**. For many cancers the risk of relapse is highest in the first two or three years and very low after five, so from then on cure can be spoken of with more confidence. In others, such as some hormone-sensitive breast cancers, late relapses can occur and follow-up is longer.

Some advanced cancers are not cured but are **controlled for years**, like a chronic disease.
