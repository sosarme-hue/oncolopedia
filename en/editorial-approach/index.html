Editorial approach

Rigour, official sources and plain language. No false hope, no alarmism.

Sources in every article

Every article cites its sources: reference bodies such as the World Health Organization and its International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), the US National Cancer Institute, health ministries, scientific societies (ESMO, ASCO, SEOM and others), patient organisations, and peer-reviewed research, above all clinical trials and guidelines.

Proven, under study, or for sale

We clearly separate what is already part of standard care, what is still being researched and what is sold without evidence. A promising headline is not an available treatment.

Figures in context

We give incidence, survival and risk figures with their source and context, and we remember that a statistic describes a group, not one person's future.

Respect for people

We avoid battle language that blames those who "lose the fight", and expressions such as "suffers from" or "victim". Nobody is to blame for their cancer. We include patient organisations as a source of support.

We do not replace your care team

Oncolopedia cannot be used to choose a treatment. We want to help you understand and ask better questions.

Update dates and corrections

Every article shows when it was last updated. Oncology moves fast: if you spot a mistake or something out of date, write to partenopides@gmail.com and we will review it.

Our network

We link to other encyclopedias in our network when it helps: Medicapedia for medicine and its history, Farmacos.ai for medicines, Geneticapedia for genetics and inherited cancer, Psycholopedia for psychology and Enciclopedias.si for all the rest.

Oncolopedia

An encyclopedia of oncology for the general public: science, health and history. It does not provide diagnosis, treatment or individual second opinions.

Have a symptom that will not go away, or worried about a diagnosis? Talk to your doctor or your cancer team. In the UK, Macmillan Support Line: 0808 808 00 00; in the US, the NCI Cancer Information Service: 1-800-4-CANCER.
