| **Risk factors** | Tobacco, obesity, new-onset diabetes, chronic pancreatitis, alcohol, inheritance (*BRCA2*, Lynch...) |
| **Signs** | **Painless jaundice**, weight loss, back pain, new diabetes |
| **Treatment** | Surgery (only 15-20%) with chemotherapy (FOLFIRINOX); chemotherapy for advanced disease |

## What it is

The **pancreas** is a gland behind the stomach with two jobs: making **digestive juices** (exocrine part) and **insulin** and other hormones (endocrine part). 90% of its cancers are **ductal adenocarcinomas**, arising in the ducts of the exocrine part. Tumours of the endocrine part are [pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours](../pancreatic-neuroendocrine-tumours/), very different and with a better outlook.

It has a dense **stroma** that hinders drug delivery, and almost always a ***KRAS*** mutation.

## Precursor lesions

- **PanIN** (pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia), microscopic.
- **Cystic neoplasms**: **intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN)** and mucinous cystadenoma, which can become malignant. They are often found by chance on CT or MRI; they are watched or removed depending on size and features (Fukuoka criteria and European guideline).

## How common it is

Its incidence is rising in Europe, and because progress has been slow, it is expected to become the **second cause of cancer death** in the EU this decade. In the UK about 10,500 people are diagnosed each year.

## Risk factors

- **Tobacco**: doubles risk; the most important avoidable cause.
- **Obesity**, inactivity, long-standing type 2 diabetes.
- **Chronic pancreatitis**, heavy alcohol use.
- **Family history** (familial pancreatic cancer: two or more first-degree relatives).
- **Inherited mutations** (10%): *BRCA2*, *BRCA1*, *PALB2*, *ATM*, Lynch syndrome, *CDKN2A* (familial melanoma), Peutz-Jeghers syndrome, hereditary pancreatitis (*PRSS1*).

## Prevention and surveillance

There is no screening for the general population. **Yearly MRI and endoscopic ultrasound surveillance** is recommended for people at high inherited or familial risk (CAPS consortium), in specialist units. Quitting smoking and keeping a healthy weight lower the risk.

## Symptoms

They appear late and are non-specific:

- **Jaundice**: yellow skin and eyes, dark urine, pale stools and itching. Typical of tumours of the **head** of the pancreas, which block the bile duct. It may be painless.
- Upper abdominal **pain** spreading to the **back**.
- **Weight loss** and poor appetite, tiredness.
- **New-onset diabetes** after 50 without obesity, or diabetes worsening for no reason.
- Fatty stools (steatorrhoea), nausea, blood clots.

## Diagnosis

1. **Pancreas-protocol CT** (arterial and venous phases): the main test; decides operability by the tumour's relation to blood vessels.
2. **Endoscopic ultrasound** with needle biopsy.
3. Liver MRI to rule out small metastases; PET-CT in some cases.
4. Blood **CA 19-9**, useful for follow-up.
5. A **biliary stent** if jaundiced.
6. **Germline genetic testing for all patients** and tumour molecular profiling (*BRCA*, *KRAS* wild-type, *NTRK*/*NRG1* fusions, MSI).

## Practical stages

| Category | Meaning | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **Resectable** | Can be operated directly | 15-20% |
| **Borderline** | Touches major vessels; chemotherapy first | 10-15% |
| **Locally advanced** | Encases arteries; not operable at first | 30% |
| **Metastatic** | Liver, peritoneum, lung | 50% |

## Treatment

**Surgery**:
- Head tumours: **pancreaticoduodenectomy (Whipple operation)**.
- Body and tail: distal pancreatectomy with splenectomy.
- It is complex surgery: results are much better in **high-volume hospitals**.

**Chemotherapy**:
- After surgery, **mFOLFIRINOX for 6 months** (PRODIGE 24 trial), with median survival over 4 years; gemcitabine with capecitabine if not tolerated.
- Borderline and locally advanced: chemotherapy first (neoadjuvant) and, if it responds, surgery.
- Metastatic disease: **FOLFIRINOX**, **NALIRIFOX** or **gemcitabine with nab-paclitaxel**.
- *BRCA* mutations: platinum and **olaparib** maintenance (POLO trial).
- MSI-H: immunotherapy. *NTRK* or *NRG1* fusions: targeted drugs.

**Supportive care**, essential:
- **Pancreatic enzymes** with meals for poor digestion.
- **Pain** control, including coeliac plexus block.
- Nutrition, diabetes control, clot prevention.
- **Early palliative care**.

## Outlook

Five-year survival is about **10%** overall, although slowly improving. In operated patients who complete chemotherapy, it can reach 25-40%.

## What's new

- **KRAS inhibitors**: daraxonrasib (RMC-6236), a "multi-selective RAS(ON)" inhibitor, and others against *KRAS* G12D; early results in metastatic disease are the most promising in decades.
- **Personalised mRNA vaccines** after surgery (autogene cevumeran study, Memorial Sloan Kettering and BioNTech).
- Early detection in people with new-onset diabetes and liquid biopsy.

## Questions for your team

- Is my tumour resectable, borderline or locally advanced?
- How many Whipple operations does this hospital do each year?
- Have I and my tumour had genetic testing?
- Do I need pancreatic enzymes?
- Is a clinical trial available?
