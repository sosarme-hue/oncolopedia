## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **How common** | Under 5% of digestive cancers |
| **Types** | Neuroendocrine tumours (ileum), adenocarcinoma (duodenum), GIST, lymphomas |
| **Risk factors** | Crohn's disease, coeliac disease, Lynch, familial adenomatous polyposis, Peutz-Jeghers |
| **Symptoms** | Colicky pain, obstruction, anaemia, bleeding |
| **Treatment** | Surgery; chemotherapy for advanced adenocarcinoma |

## What it is

The **small bowel** (duodenum, jejunum and ileum) is several metres long, yet its cancers are rare. The four main types behave very differently:

- **Neuroendocrine tumours**: the most common, mainly in the ileum; see [gastrointestinal neuroendocrine tumours](../gastrointestinal-neuroendocrine-tumours/).
- **Adenocarcinoma**: mainly in the duodenum; treated similarly to colon cancer.
- **GIST**: see [its sheet](../gastrointestinal-stromal-tumour-gist/).
- **Lymphomas**: including enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma in poorly controlled coeliac disease.

## Symptoms and diagnosis

Abdominal pain that comes and goes, nausea, **bowel obstruction**, anaemia, hidden bleeding, weight loss. Because it is hard to examine, diagnosis is often delayed. Tests: CT or MR enterography, **capsule endoscopy**, balloon enteroscopy, upper endoscopy for the duodenum.

## Treatment

- **Surgery** removing the segment and its nodes; in the duodenum, sometimes a pancreaticoduodenectomy (Whipple operation).
- **Adenocarcinoma**: adjuvant chemotherapy (FOLFOX or CAPOX) when nodes are involved, by analogy with colon cancer; in advanced disease, chemotherapy and immunotherapy if microsatellite-unstable.

## Outlook

It depends on the type: excellent for localised neuroendocrine tumours, intermediate for adenocarcinoma (about 35-40% at five years overall).
