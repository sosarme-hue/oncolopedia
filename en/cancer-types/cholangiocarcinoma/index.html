Cancer types

Cholangiocarcinoma

Cancer of the bile ducts, inside or outside the liver. It is uncommon and hard to treat, but many carry genetic alterations with targeted treatments.

General information only. It does not replace advice from your care team and should not be used to choose a treatment.

At a glance
How common 1-2 cases per 100,000 people a year in Europe; much higher in South-East Asia
Types Intrahepatic, perihilar (Klatskin tumour) and distal
Risk factors Primary sclerosing cholangitis, choledochal cysts, liver flukes, intrahepatic stones, cirrhosis
Typical sign Jaundice (in extrahepatic tumours)
Treatment Surgery with capecitabine; chemo-immunotherapy; targeted therapy (FGFR2, IDH1, HER2)

What it is

The bile ducts carry bile from the liver to the bowel. Cholangiocarcinoma is classified by where it starts:

  • Intrahepatic: inside the liver; appears as a liver mass. It is increasing.
  • Perihilar (Klatskin tumour): where the right and left ducts join; the most common.
  • Distal: near the pancreas; operated like pancreatic cancer (Whipple).

Together with gallbladder cancer they form the biliary tract cancers.

Risk factors

Primary sclerosing cholangitis (often with ulcerative colitis), choledochal cysts, intrahepatic stones, liver flukes (Opisthorchis, Clonorchis, from eating raw freshwater fish in Thailand and elsewhere), hepatitis B and C, cirrhosis, fatty liver, obesity and diabetes.

Symptoms and diagnosis

Jaundice with itching, dark urine and pale stools in extrahepatic tumours; pain, weight loss and a mass in intrahepatic ones. Tests include CT, MRCP, ERCP or cholangioscopy with brushings and biopsy, endoscopic ultrasound and CA 19-9. Molecular profiling is essential in advanced disease: about 40% of intrahepatic tumours have a treatable alteration.

Treatment

  • Surgery (hepatectomy, bile duct resection, Whipple), the only curative option, possible in 20-30%.
  • Capecitabine for 6 months after surgery (BILCAP trial).
  • Liver transplant for early inoperable perihilar tumours, in selected centres (Mayo Clinic protocol).
  • Advanced disease: gemcitabine and cisplatin with durvalumab (TOPAZ-1 trial) or pembrolizumab (KEYNOTE-966).
  • Targeted therapy: FGFR2 fusions (pemigatinib, futibatinib), IDH1 mutations (ivosidenib), HER2 (zanidatamab, trastuzumab deruxtecan), BRAF V600E (dabrafenib and trametinib), NTRK, MSI-H.
  • Biliary drainage with stents for jaundice; radioembolisation or radiotherapy for intrahepatic disease.

Outlook

Five-year survival is 10-20% overall, and 25-40% after complete surgery.
